About English

English is an important language for communication, learning and expressing our thoughts clearly. Studying English helps students improve their reading, writing, speaking and listening skills. It also introduces them to new ideas, different experiences and a variety of writing styles through stories, poems and other literary works.

Learning English is not just about memorising grammar rules or learning difficult words. It is about understanding what we read, expressing our ideas in the right words and communicating confidently with others. Whether a student is writing an answer, reading a story or having a conversation, these skills are useful in everyday life.

Major Areas of English

Area What Students Learn Everyday Use
Reading Understanding passages, stories, poems and other texts. Reading books, notices and instructions.
Writing Writing paragraphs, letters, essays and answers clearly. Writing messages, emails and applications.
Grammar Understanding sentence structure, tenses and correct usage. Speaking and writing more accurately.
Literature Understanding characters, themes, poems and stories. Developing imagination and understanding different experiences.
Vocabulary Learning new words, meanings and their correct use. Expressing thoughts more clearly and confidently.

Why Is English Important for Students?

  • Improves communication and expression.
  • Helps students understand textbooks and learning materials.
  • Develops reading comprehension and writing skills.
  • Builds confidence in conversations and presentations.
  • Supports preparation for higher studies, interviews and many competitive examinations.

Remember: English improves with regular use. Read a little every day, learn new words in context and practise expressing your thoughts in your own words.

Importance of the NCERT English Book

NCERT English textbooks give students an opportunity to improve their language skills while exploring stories, poems, conversations and other forms of writing. The lessons introduce different themes and situations that help students understand how language is used to express ideas, feelings and experiences.

Reading the textbook carefully is important because examination questions often require students to understand the lesson, explain its meaning and support their answers with relevant details. Simply memorising question-and-answer sets may not prepare students to handle questions asked in a different way.

How the NCERT English Book Helps

Textbook Feature How It Helps Students
Stories and Prose Improves reading comprehension and understanding of characters, events and themes.
Poems Helps students explore meaning, emotions, imagery and poetic expression.
Textbook Questions Provides practice in understanding lessons and writing relevant answers.
Grammar and Writing Tasks Helps students improve sentence formation and written communication, where included in the course.

How to Study English from NCERT

  1. Read the lesson carefully and understand its main idea.
  2. Note important words and learn their meanings in context.
  3. Identify the characters, events, themes or central message.
  4. Attempt textbook questions in your own words.
  5. Revise grammar and practise writing according to your syllabus.

Study tip: After reading a story or poem, try explaining its main idea without looking at the book. This is a simple way to check whether you have really understood the lesson.

A Practical Study Timetable for English

English is best learned through regular reading, writing and revision. Students do not need to study only before examinations. A little daily practice can gradually improve vocabulary, comprehension, grammar and confidence in writing.

The following is a suggested daily routine. Adjust the timings according to your school schedule, homework and learning needs.

Duration Study Activity Purpose
15 minutes Read a textbook passage, story or poem. Improve reading and comprehension.
15 minutes Learn new words and revise their meanings. Build vocabulary.
20 minutes Practise grammar exercises or sentence formation. Improve language accuracy.
25 minutes Write a textbook answer, paragraph or other writing task. Develop clear writing skills.
10 minutes Check spelling, grammar and mistakes. Improve accuracy.
Weekend: 45–60 minutes Revise lessons and attempt a practice paper or writing task. Review progress and build confidence.

Tips for Better English Preparation

  • Read English regularly, even if it is only a few pages a day.
  • Learn new words with example sentences instead of memorising meanings alone.
  • Practise writing answers in clear and simple language.
  • Revise grammar rules by using them in sentences.
  • Read your written answers again to check spelling and sentence structure.

Remember: You do not need to use difficult words to write a good answer. Focus on understanding the question and expressing your ideas clearly.

Frequently Asked Questions About English

1. How can I improve my English?

Read regularly, learn new words in context and practise writing short paragraphs. Try to use English in everyday conversations and gradually build your confidence.

2. How can I improve my English vocabulary?

Learn a few new words every day. Write their meanings, make example sentences and revise them regularly. Reading stories and articles is also a useful way to discover new vocabulary.

3. Do I need to memorise textbook answers?

It is more useful to understand the lesson and practise explaining it in your own words. Remember important details, but avoid depending entirely on memorised answers.

4. How can I write better answers in English?

Read the question carefully, identify what it asks and organise your ideas before writing. Use clear sentences, relevant details and correct grammar.

5. How should I prepare for English grammar?

Understand the rules and practise them through exercises. Write your own sentences using the rules and review mistakes to understand where you need more practice.

6. How can I understand English poems?

Read the poem slowly, understand the meanings of unfamiliar words and identify its central idea. Pay attention to the poet's feelings, imagery and message where relevant.

7. How much time should I study English daily?

A focused session of around 45–90 minutes can be a useful starting point for many students. Adjust the duration according to your syllabus, learning needs and other subjects.

8. How can I improve my reading comprehension?

Read the passage carefully, identify its main idea and look for details that support your understanding. Practise answering questions based on the passage rather than guessing.

9. What should I do if I make spelling or grammar mistakes?

Keep a small list of repeated mistakes and revise them regularly. Read your writing aloud, check sentence structure and practise the correct form in new sentences.

10. How should I revise English before an examination?

Revise the prescribed lessons, important themes, vocabulary and grammar topics. Practise writing answers and complete sample papers where applicable. Leave time to check spelling and grammar.
