Check Your Understanding (Part I)

I. Do you think pot making is easy? If yes, why? If no, why not?

Answer:

No, pot making is not easy. It requires great skill, patience, hard work, and continuous practice. A potter has to collect clay from the riverbank, soften and prepare it, shape it carefully on the wheel, dry it properly, and fire it in a kiln. Even a small mistake in shaping or firing can spoil the entire batch of pots.

Yes, Sentila would be able to fulfil her dream. She was passionate about pot making and never gave up despite facing many difficulties. She secretly observed expert potters, practised regularly, and remained determined to learn the craft. Her dedication and perseverance eventually helped her become a skilled pot maker.

II. Answer the following questions.

1. Describe the process of pot making followed by expert pot makers, as observed by Sentila.

Answer:

Sentila observed that expert pot makers first mixed the clay with water and softened it by pounding it. They then skillfully shaped the clay using their hands and a spatula. After giving the pots their final shape, they dried them in the sun and finally fired them carefully in a kiln. Every step required patience, precision, and experience.

2. What warning was given to Mesoba by the village council?

Answer:

The village council reminded Mesoba that it was Arenla's responsibility to pass on the traditional skill of pot making to Sentila. They warned that such skills belonged to the community and should be taught to future generations so that the tradition would not disappear.

3. How did Sentila feel when she failed at pot making even after a year of training with her mother?

Answer:

Sentila felt ashamed, frustrated, and disappointed. Despite practising for a year, she could not make a proper pot, but she did not lose hope and continued trying to achieve her dream.

4. 'Onula stood there for a long time as if trying to absorb a new phenomenon.' Explain.

Answer:

Onula saw two identical rows of freshly made pots and realised that Sentila had become as skilled as her mother. The pots were so similar that she could not distinguish between them. This remarkable achievement filled her with wonder, and she understood that a new master pot maker had emerged.

5. 'The tradition and history of the people did not belong to any individual.' What does this symbolise?

Answer:

This statement symbolises that traditional knowledge and cultural heritage are the collective wealth of society. Such skills should be preserved and passed on to future generations rather than being kept as personal property.

6. What is the significance of the concluding line of the story, 'A new pot maker was born'?

Answer:

The concluding line signifies Sentila's transformation into a skilled pot maker. It marks the success of her hard work, determination, and perseverance. It also symbolises the continuation of a valuable traditional craft from one generation to the next.

7. What is the role of perseverance in pursuing one's dreams? Elaborate with reference to Sentila.

Answer:

Perseverance is the key to achieving success despite failures and difficulties. Sentila never gave up on her dream of becoming a pot maker. She continued observing expert potters, practised patiently, accepted guidance from Onula, and learnt from her mistakes. Her determination and constant effort finally enabled her to master the art of pot making, proving that sincere hard work and perseverance always lead to success.

What is a Noun Clause?

A noun clause is a group of words that contains a subject and a verb and functions as a noun in a sentence. It can act as the subject, object, or complement of the main clause.

Examples:

  • I know that she is honest.
  • He explained why he was late.
  • We believe that hard work leads to success.

What is a Relative Clause?

A relative clause (also called an adjectival clause) gives additional information about a noun or pronoun in the main clause. It usually begins with a relative pronoun such as who, whom, whose, which, or that, or a relative adverb such as where or when.

Examples

  • The girl who won the competition received a prize.
  • This is the workshop where the potters make clay pots.
  • I read a story which inspired me to work hard.
  • The teacher whose guidance helped me is highly respected.

Remember

  • Who → Refers to people.
  • Whose → Shows possession.
  • Which → Refers to things or animals.
  • That → Refers to people or things.
  • Where → Refers to a place.
  • When → Refers to time.
