Critical Reflection

I. Read the extracts given below and answer the questions that follow.

1. Extract

"I had two choices—squander my life in remorse or transform it to a world of limitless possibilities.

I love sports and had been a swimmer too, so I decided to switch to para-athletics. This is how my Paralympics journey began. My breakthrough moment came in the 2016 Rio Paralympic Games, when I secured the silver medal in the shot-put event. In hindsight, I feel it was a moment of personal victory and a step forward in changing perceptions."

(i) Give a reason for the following statement.

Question: The speaker’s decision to transform her life can be likened to the concept of a caterpillar transforming into a butterfly.

Answer: Just as a caterpillar transforms into a beautiful butterfly after overcoming difficulties, Dr. Deepa Malik transformed her life by refusing to give up after becoming paralysed. She accepted her challenges with courage, entered para-athletics, and became an inspiration to millions.

(ii) Why could the speaker switch to para-athletics quite comfortably?

Answer: The speaker could switch to para-athletics comfortably because she had always loved sports and had been a swimmer before her illness. Her interest in sports helped her begin a successful career in para-athletics.

(iii) Complete the sentence with an appropriate reason.

Question: The speaker calls the 2016 Rio Paralympic Games a "breakthrough moment" because __________.

Answer: she won the silver medal in the shot-put event, making it a personal victory and helping to change people's perceptions about disability.

(iv) Select the correct option to complete the sentence.

Question: The phrase "in hindsight" indicates the speaker is __________.

  • A. curious
  • B. reflective ✔
  • C. determined
  • D. courageous

Answer: Option B. Reflective

Explanation: "In hindsight" means looking back and thinking about past experiences carefully.

(v) The speaker uses the phrase "changing perceptions". List one likely perception the speaker might have changed.

Answer: She changed the belief that people with disabilities cannot achieve success in sports. Through her achievements, she proved that determination and hard work are more important than physical limitations.

2. Extract

"Honestly, I feel sports, especially Paralympics, have the extraordinary ability to challenge stereotypes and change attitudes towards disability. When people witness the strength, skill, and competitive spirit of para-athletes, it breaks down preconceived notions. Paralympics has given me a new lease of life and helped me push boundaries."

(i) Choose the option that lists the words to describe the tone of the speaker.

  • A. Appreciative
  • B. Moralistic
  • C. Sentimental
  • D. Optimistic
  • E. Defensive

Answer: (a) A and D ✔

Explanation: The speaker appreciates the role of sports and expresses optimism about changing society's attitude towards disability.

(ii) Complete the analogy.

Question: ability : potential :: preconceived notions : __________

Answer: stereotypes

(iii) State whether the following sentence is True or False.

Statement: Paralympics is a platform that not only showcases the remarkable abilities of para-athletes but also questions stereotypes.

Answer: True.

(iv) What does "helped me push boundaries" tell us about the speaker?

Answer: It tells us that the speaker became stronger, more confident, and achieved goals that many people thought were impossible. She refused to accept limits and kept moving forward with determination.

(v) Which phrase from the extract suggests that the speaker was able to transform her life?

Answer: "given me a new lease of life"

II. Answer the following questions.

1. Explain how Dr. Malik’s achievements challenge societal perceptions.

Answer: Dr. Deepa Malik's achievements prove that physical disabilities do not limit a person's talent or determination. By winning medals and receiving prestigious awards, she challenged the common belief that people with disabilities cannot excel in sports. Her success has inspired society to respect and appreciate the abilities of para-athletes.

2. What can be the long-term impact of involving youth in schools and colleges in advocacy with respect to disability?

Answer: Involving young people in disability advocacy can create a more inclusive and compassionate society. It helps students understand the importance of equality, respect, and accessibility. In the long run, they will support the rights of persons with disabilities and help remove discrimination from society.

3. Rationalise the appropriateness of the title of this text with reference to Dr. Deepa Malik’s attitude and achievements.

Answer: The title The World of Limitless Possibilities is appropriate because Dr. Deepa Malik never allowed her disability to stop her from achieving success. She turned challenges into opportunities, won international medals, and inspired countless people. Her life shows that determination and confidence can overcome every obstacle.

4. "Every setback is an opportunity to prove your strength." How might this be a life lesson for every individual, and not just sportspersons?

Answer: This message teaches that failures and difficulties should not discourage us. Every challenge helps us become stronger, wiser, and more determined. Whether in studies, work, or personal life, people who face problems with courage can achieve success and inspire others.

5. Examine how Dr. Deepa Malik’s recognition as one of the 10 most inspirational para-athletes globally contributes to the larger discourse on gender equality in sports.

Answer: Dr. Deepa Malik's global recognition proves that women, including women with disabilities, can achieve excellence in sports. Her success encourages equal opportunities for female athletes and inspires girls to participate in sports without fear. It strengthens the message that talent and determination matter more than gender or disability.

6. How might Dr. Deepa Malik’s thought, "ability beyond disability", serve as a guideline for success for all future para-athletes?

Answer: The idea "ability beyond disability" encourages para-athletes to focus on their strengths instead of their limitations. It teaches them to believe in themselves, work hard, and never lose confidence. This positive attitude can help them achieve success and become role models for others.

7. What have you learnt from this interview, and how can you implement these learnings in your life?

Answer: This interview teaches us to face every challenge with courage, determination, and self-confidence. It reminds us that success depends on hard work and a positive attitude rather than physical limitations. I will apply these lessons by staying confident during difficult situations, working sincerely towards my goals, and respecting everyone equally.

Nine Gold Medals

I. Read the extracts given below and answer the questions that follow.

Extract 1

The eight other runners pulled up on their heels The ones who had trained for so long to compete One by one they all turned round and went back to help him And brought the young boy to his feet.




(i) Answer: Option B. Stopped running

Explanation: The phrase "pulled up on their heels" means that the runners immediately stopped running after seeing the young athlete fall.

(ii) Answer:

One character trait common to all the eight runners is compassion. They also showed kindness, empathy, selflessness, and true sportsmanship by helping the fallen athlete instead of continuing the race.

(iii) Answer:

The tone of the poet in these lines is appreciative, emotional, and inspiring. The poet admires the runners for choosing kindness over competition.

(iv) Answer:

The young athlete might have felt grateful, relieved, encouraged, and deeply touched. The support of the other runners gave him confidence and made him realise that friendship and humanity are more important than winning.

(v) Answer:

Yes, this incident is the turning point of the poem because it changes the race from a competition into an unforgettable example of compassion and unity. From this moment, the focus shifts from winning medals to helping another person.

Extract 2

That’s how the race ended, with nine gold medals They came to the finish line holding hands still And a standing ovation and nine beaming faces Said more than these words ever will.




(i) Answer:

The nine contestants felt happy, proud, satisfied, and united when they reached the finishing line together. Their smiling faces reflected the joy of achieving success through kindness and teamwork.

(ii) Answer:

All the nine contestants were given gold medals because they displayed the true spirit of sportsmanship. They showed compassion, unity, and humanity by helping the fallen athlete instead of thinking only about victory.

(iii) Answer:

The holding of hands signifies a feeling of unity, friendship, cooperation, mutual support, and togetherness.

(iv) Answer: Option A. Amazed

Explanation: The spectators gave a standing ovation because they were deeply impressed by the athletes' kindness, teamwork, and selfless behaviour.

(v) Answer:

The last line means that the smiling faces of the athletes and the audience's standing ovation expressed emotions that words could not fully describe. Their actions demonstrated the values of empathy, kindness, unity, and true sportsmanship more powerfully than any speech.

II. Answer the following questions.

1. Describe how the setting established in the first two stanzas of the poem create a vivid atmosphere for the events that follow.

Answer:

The first two stanzas create an atmosphere of excitement, enthusiasm, and anticipation. Athletes from different parts of the country gather after months of hard training with the hope of winning gold, silver, or bronze medals. Spectators eagerly surround the field, cheering for the participants and waiting for the final event to begin. This energetic setting prepares the readers for an exciting race. However, the unexpected act of kindness shown later by the athletes creates a powerful contrast, making the poem even more touching and memorable.

2. How do you think the youngest athlete might have felt when he fell?

Answer:

The youngest athlete must have felt shocked, disappointed, and heartbroken when he fell. After spending many weeks and months preparing for the race, he saw his dream of winning a medal disappear in a single moment. He probably felt helpless and frustrated because he could not continue the race. However, when the other athletes returned to help him, his disappointment changed into gratitude, happiness, and hope.

3. Why were the athletes eager to begin the race?

Answer:

The athletes were eager to begin the race because they had worked very hard for many weeks and months to prepare for the competition. Each participant wanted to perform well and fulfil the dream of winning a medal. The race was the final event of the day, making everyone even more excited and determined to give their best performance.

4. What does the transformation of the hundred-yard dash to a walk symbolise?

Answer:

The transformation of the hundred-yard dash into a walk symbolises humanity, compassion, unity, and true sportsmanship. Instead of competing against one another, the athletes chose to help a fellow runner who had fallen. Their action shows that kindness, cooperation, and empathy are more valuable than individual success or winning a race.

5. How might the poem be different if the focus was solely on individual achievement rather than collective support?

Answer:

If the poem focused only on individual achievement, it would simply describe a competitive race and the victory of one athlete. It would lose its emotional impact and inspiring message. The poem becomes unique because it highlights compassion, teamwork, and selflessness. It teaches readers that helping others is a greater achievement than winning medals.

6. How does the poet's use of language and tone enhance the reader's engagement with the poem?

Answer:

The poet uses simple, vivid, and expressive language that allows readers to imagine every scene clearly. Descriptive words and imagery create excitement at the beginning, sadness when the young athlete falls, and joy when the runners help him. The tone changes from suspenseful to emotional and finally to inspiring. This keeps readers interested throughout the poem and helps them understand the importance of kindness and sportsmanship.

7. What might be the poet's purpose of writing this poem?

Answer:

The poet's purpose is to show that compassion, empathy, and humanity are more important than winning a competition. Through the actions of the nine athletes, the poet highlights the true spirit of the Special Olympics, where helping others and showing respect are considered greater achievements than individual victory. The poem encourages readers to value kindness, teamwork, and selfless behaviour in their own lives.
