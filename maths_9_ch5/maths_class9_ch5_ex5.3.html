Question 1 — Can you explain why the converse to Theorem 4 is true, i.e., why does the perpendicular from the centre of a circle to a chord of the circle bisect the chord?

Let $AB$ be a chord with centre $O$. Suppose $OM\perp AB$.

In $\triangle OMA$ and $\triangle OMB$:

  • $OA=OB$ — radii.
  • $OM=OM$ — common side.
  • $\angle OMA=\angle OMB=90^\circ$.

Hence the triangles are congruent by RHS. Therefore, $AM=BM$ by CPCT.

Answer: The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord.

Question 2 — An isosceles triangle ABC is inscribed in a circle, with AB = AC. Show that the altitude from A to BC passes through the centre of the circle.

Let $AB=AC$ and let $AD$ be the altitude to $BC$. Then $AD\perp BC$.

In $\triangle ABD$ and $\triangle ACD$, $AB=AC$, $AD$ is common and both angles at $D$ are right angles. Hence the triangles are congruent by RHS.

So $BD=DC$. Thus $AD$ is the perpendicular bisector of chord $BC$. The perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the centre.

Answer: The altitude $AD$ passes through the centre of the circle.

Question 3 — Two parallel chords of lengths 6 cm and 8 cm are on opposite sides of the centre of a circle. If the radius of the circle is 5 cm, find the distance between the midpoints of the chords.

Given: radius $=5$ cm; chord lengths $6$ cm and $8$ cm, on opposite sides of the centre.

The perpendicular from the centre bisects each chord. Hence the half-chords are $3$ cm and $4$ cm.

Draw two perpendiculars OM1 and OM2 respectively from the centre to the chords.

By Pythagoras theorem in the right triangles formed by the radius, half-chord and perpendicular distance from the centre to the chord, we have:

M1O = √(52 − 32) = √(25 − 9) = 4 cm


M2O = √(52 − 42) = √(25 − 16) = 3 cm

Distance between the midpoints of the chords = 4 + 3 = 7 cm.
