Q1. In a circle, a chord is 5 cm away from the centre. If the radius of the circle is 13 cm, what is the length of the chord?

Solution:

Given:

Radius of the circle, OA = 13 cm

Perpendicular distance of the chord from the centre, OM = 5 cm

Let AB be the chord and OM be perpendicular to the chord AB.

Since the perpendicular drawn from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord,

AM = MB

In right-angled triangle OMA, by Pythagoras theorem,

OA2 = OM2 + AM2

Therefore,

AM2 = OA2 − OM2

= 132 − 52

= 169 − 25

= 144

Therefore, AM = 12 cm.

Since AM = MB,

AB = AM + MB

= 12 + 12

= 24 cm

Therefore, the length of the chord is 24 cm.

Q2. An arc of a circle subtends an angle of 70° at the centre. What is the measure of the angle subtended by the arc at a point on the circle?

Solution:

Let the arc AB of a circle subtend an angle of 70° at the centre O.

Let the same arc AB subtend an angle ACB at a point C on the circle.

We know that:

The angle subtended by an arc at the centre is twice the angle subtended by it at any point on the remaining part of the circle.

Therefore,

∠AOB = 2 × ∠ACB

Given,

∠AOB = 70°

Therefore,

70° = 2 × ∠ACB

∠ACB = 70°/ 2

∠ACB = 35°

Therefore, the measure of the angle subtended by the arc at a point on the circle is 35°.

Q10. A cyclic quadrilateral has sides 5, 5, 12, 12 units. Find its area.

A cyclic quadrilateral has sides 5 cm, 5 cm, 12 cm and 12 cm. Find its area.

Solution

Let the sides of the cyclic quadrilateral be:

a = 5 cm,   b = 5 cm,   c = 12 cm,   d = 12 cm

For a cyclic quadrilateral, we use Brahmagupta's formula:

Area = √[(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)(s − d)]

where s is the semiperimeter.

Therefore,

s = (a + b + c + d) / 2

= (5 + 5 + 12 + 12) / 2

= 34 / 2

= 17 cm

Now, using Brahmagupta's formula:

Area = √[(17 − 5)(17 − 5)(17 − 12)(17 − 12)]

= √(12 × 12 × 5 × 5)

= √(144 × 25)

= √3600

= 60 cm2

Therefore, the area of the cyclic quadrilateral is 60 cm2.

Q11. Consider a cyclic quadrilateral. Without drawing its circumcircle, how can we find out whether the centre of the circumcircle lies inside the quadrilateral or outside? What is the best way of finding out?

Let ABCD be a cyclic quadrilateral. We want to determine whether the centre of its circumcircle lies inside or outside the quadrilateral, without drawing the circle.

The best way is to examine the interior angles of the cyclic quadrilateral.

For a cyclic quadrilateral, opposite angles are supplementary:

∠A + ∠C = 180°

and

∠B + ∠D = 180°

Now consider the following cases.

Case 1: All angles are less than 90°

If all the angles are less than 90°, the circumcentre lies inside the quadrilateral.

Case 2: One angle is exactly 90°

If one angle is 90°, the side opposite that angle is a diameter of the circumcircle, by the converse of Thales' theorem. Therefore, the circumcentre lies on the corresponding diagonal of the quadrilateral and hence is still inside the quadrilateral.

For example, if:

∠A = 90°

then diagonal BD is a diameter, and the circumcentre lies on diagonal BD.

Case 3: One angle is greater than 90°

If any angle is greater than 90°, the circumcentre lies outside the quadrilateral.

For example, if:

∠A > 90°

then the circumcentre lies outside ABCD.

Conclusion

Therefore, without drawing the circumcircle, we can determine the position of its centre by examining the angles of the cyclic quadrilateral:

If all four angles are less than or equal to 90°, the circumcentre lies inside the quadrilateral.

If any one angle is greater than 90°, the circumcentre lies outside the quadrilateral.

Hence,

Circumcentre inside  →  All angles ≤ 90°

Circumcentre outside  →  At least one angle > 90°

Q16. Consider all chords of a circle of a fixed length. What is the shape formed by the midpoints of all these chords?

Let the radius of the given circle be R and let the fixed length of each chord be l.

Consider any chord AB of the given circle and let M be its midpoint. Let O be the centre of the circle.

Since the perpendicular drawn from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord, we have:

OM ⊥ AB

and

AM = MB = l/2

In the right-angled triangle OMA, by Pythagoras' theorem:

OA2 = OM2 + AM2

Therefore,

OM2 = OA2 - AM2

= R2 - (l/2)2

Hence,

OM = √[R2 - l2/4]

Here, R and l are fixed quantities. Therefore, OM is constant for every chord of the given fixed length.

Thus, the midpoint M of every such chord remains at a constant distance from the fixed point O.

The locus of a point which remains at a constant distance from a fixed point is a circle.

Therefore, the midpoints of all chords of a fixed length form a circle having the same centre as the given circle.

The radius of this circle is:

√[R2 - l2/4]

Hence, the shape formed by the midpoints of all chords of a fixed length is a circle concentric with the given circle.

Q21. Let ABCD be a cyclic quadrilateral. Explain why the exterior angle at any vertex is equal to the interior opposite angle.

Let ABCD be a cyclic quadrilateral and let CD be extended to E.

We have to prove that the exterior angle ∠EDA is equal to the interior opposite angle ∠ABC.

In a cyclic quadrilateral, the sum of a pair of opposite angles is 180°.

Therefore,

∠CDA + ∠ABC = 180°

Since CD is extended to E, the angles ∠CDA and ∠EDA form a linear pair.

Therefore,

∠CDA + ∠EDA = 180°

From the above two equations,

∠CDA + ∠ABC = ∠CDA + ∠EDA

Subtracting ∠CDA from both sides, we get

∠EDA = ∠ABC

Hence, the exterior angle of a cyclic quadrilateral is equal to its interior opposite angle.

∴ ∠EDA = ∠ABC

Q22. “There is no chord of a circle that is longer than its diameter.” How do you justify this statement?

Let AB be any chord of a circle with centre O. Join OA and OB. Also, draw OM perpendicular to the chord AB.

Since the perpendicular from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord, AM = MB.

In right-angled triangle OMA,

OA2 = OM2 + AM2

Therefore,

AM < OA

Hence,

AB = 2AM < 2OA

But 2OA is the diameter of the circle. Therefore,

AB < Diameter

The only chord that passes through the centre is the diameter itself. In that case, the chord has the maximum possible length.

Thus, no chord of a circle can be longer than its diameter.

∴ The diameter is the longest chord of a circle.

Q24. How would you use the following figure to justify the statement that the angle in a semicircle is 90°?

Let the endpoints of the diameter be B and C, and let A be any point on the semicircle. Join OA.

Since OA, OB and OC are radii of the same circle,

OA = OB and OA = OC

Therefore, ▵AOB and ▵AOC are isosceles triangles.

In ▵AOB, the two base angles are equal. Hence, if the angle at B is b, then

∠BAO = b

Similarly, in ▵AOC, if the angle at C is a, then

∠CAO = a

Since BC is a diameter, B, O, C are collinear. Therefore,

∠BOC = 180°

The angles at O in the two isosceles triangles are therefore:

∠AOB = 180° - 2b

∠AOC = 180° - 2a

Since these two angles together form a straight angle,

(180° - 2a) + (180° - 2b) = 180°

Therefore,

a + b = 90°

Now consider ▵ABC. Its angle at A is divided by AO into the angles a and b.

Hence,

∠A = a + b = 90°

Thus, the angle subtended by a diameter at any point on the semicircle is a right angle.

∴ The angle in a semicircle is 90°.

Q26. How would you use the following figure to justify the statement that the sum of the opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral is 180°?

Let ABCD be a cyclic quadrilateral with centre O. From the figure, AC and BD are diameters of the circle.

Since OA, OB, OC and OD are radii of the same circle,

OA = OB = OC = OD

Consider ▵AOB

Since OA = OB, ▵AOB is an isosceles triangle. Therefore, its base angles are equal.

p = q

Consider ▵COD

Since OC = OD, ▵COD is an isosceles triangle. Therefore, its base angles are equal.

u = v

Using the diameters

Since AC is a diameter, A, O, C are collinear. Hence,

∠AOD + ∠DOC = 180°

In ▵AOD, since OA = OD,

∠AOD = 180° - 2v

Similarly, in ▵DOC, since OD = OC,

∠DOC = 180° - 2u

Therefore,

(180° - 2v) + (180° - 2u) = 180°

Hence,

u + v = 90°

Now consider the opposite angles

The interior angle at A is divided by AO into the angles p and v. Therefore,

∠A = p + v

Similarly, the interior angle at C is divided by CO into the angles u and q. Therefore,

∠C = u + q

But we have already established that p = q and u = v. Therefore,

∠A + ∠C = p + v + u + q

= 2p + 2u

Also, since BD is a diameter, the corresponding right-angle relationships give

p + u = 90°

Therefore,

∠A + ∠C = 2(90°) = 180°

∴ The sum of the opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral is 180°.

Similarly,

∠B + ∠D = 180°
