Chapter Overview

Chapter 10 studies how to combine data fairly when groups contain different numbers of observations. It connects arithmetic mean with weighted averages, mixtures, proportions and stacked bar charts.

These worked solutions show the mathematical reasoning behind selected exercise questions and end-of-chapter problems.

Textbook-check note: Use the exact question numbers and diagrams in your copy of Ganita Manjari. Where data are read from a graph, answers are marked as estimates. This page does not reproduce every printed exercise verbatim.

Important Concepts

ConceptExplanation
Combined averageAdd the totals from all groups and divide by the total number of observations.
Weighted averageMultiply each group’s mean by its group size, add these products, and divide by the total size.
Mean depth of a poolFor sections of unequal lengths, use a length-weighted average of the depths.
Stacked bar chartEach bar represents a whole, while segments show the component quantities.
Percentage in a 100% stacked barDivide a component total by the overall total and multiply by 100.

Exercise Solutions — Chapter 10

Exercise Set 10.1 — Combining averages

Question 1 — Combined class average

Section A has 30 students with average score 72; Section B has 25 students with average 76.

Total marks in A = 30 × 72 = 2160.

Total marks in B = 25 × 76 = 1900.

Combined average = (2160 + 1900) ÷ (30 + 25) = 4060 ÷ 55 ≈ 73.82 marks.

Question 2 — Equal quantities in a mixture

Three equal amounts contain nitrogen fractions 1/10, 9/50 and 3/60.

Convert to hundredths: 1/10 = 10/100, 9/50 = 18/100, 3/60 = 5/100.

Mean fraction = (10/100 + 18/100 + 5/100) ÷ 3 = 33/100 ÷ 3 = 11/100 = 11%.

Exercise Set 10.2–10.4 — Applying weighted averages

Question — Shares bought at different prices

A person owns 25 shares at an average price of ₹150 and buys 10 more at ₹30 each.

Original cost = 25 × 150 = ₹3750. New cost = 10 × 30 = ₹300.

Combined cost = ₹4050; total shares = 35. Average price = 4050 ÷ 35 = ₹115.71 per share (approximately).

Question — Pool divided into sections of different lengths

A 30-hasta pool is divided into lengths 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8 hastas, with depths 9, 7, 7, 3 and 2 hastas.

Weighted sum of depths = 4×9 + 5×7 + 6×7 + 7×3 + 8×2 = 36 + 35 + 42 + 21 + 16 = 150.

Mean depth = 150 ÷ 30 = 5 hastas.

Question — Doubling all weights

Let a weighted mean be M = (w₁x₁ + … + wₙxₙ)/(w₁ + … + wₙ). Doubling each weight gives M′ = (2w₁x₁ + … + 2wₙxₙ)/(2w₁ + … + 2wₙ). Cancelling 2 from numerator and denominator gives M′ = M. The weighted average does not change.

Exercise Set 10.5 — Reading stacked bar charts

Question — Daily activity percentages

To convert hours into a percentage of the day, calculate (hours ÷ 24) × 100. For example, 8 hours lying down gives (8/24)×100 = 33⅓%, 10 hours sitting gives 41⅔%, and 6 hours standing/moving gives 25%. These add to 100%.

On a 100% stacked bar, the cumulative boundaries are 33⅓%, 75% and 100%.

Question — Space objects shown in a graph

Readings from the published graph are approximate: the total in 2003 is about 10,000, so it doubles to about 20,000 around 2021. In 2025, the graph suggests about 33,000 objects, including about 17,000 payload objects and 16,000 other objects. Payload share ≈ 17,000/33,000×100 ≈ 52%; other share ≈ 48%.

These figures are estimates taken from the graph, not exact counts.

End-of-Chapter Exercises

Question 1 — Cricket run rate

(i) Total runs after two overs = 6 + 12 = 18. Run rate = 18 ÷ 2 = 9 runs per over.

(ii) After 19 overs: 19 × 6 = 114 runs. Add 12 in the final over: 126 runs. Run rate after 20 overs = 126 ÷ 20 = 6.3 runs per over.

Question 2 — Shuttlecock prices

The five friends collected 6, 4, 5, 4 and 4 prices, respectively: 23 prices altogether. If their means are aᵧ, aₛ, aₖ, aₚ and a𝗀, then the combined mean is (6aᵧ + 4aₛ + 5aₖ + 4aₚ + 4a𝗀)/23.

Their sums are ₹917, ₹493, ₹766, ₹897 and ₹990. Combined average = (917 + 493 + 766 + 897 + 990) ÷ 23 = 4063 ÷ 23 ≈ ₹176.65.

Grouped by type, 13 prices are for nylon (total ₹1429) and 10 for feather (total ₹2634); (1429 + 2634) ÷ 23 gives the same mean, ₹176.65.

Question 3 — Average price of shares

(i) Original cost = 25×₹150 = ₹3750. Ten shares at ₹30 cost ₹300. New average = ₹4050/35 = ₹115.71 approximately.

(ii) If Vaishnavi had 5 shares at ₹150 and buys x shares at ₹30, her new average is ₹70:

(750 + 30x)/(5 + x) = 70 ⇒ 750 + 30x = 350 + 70x ⇒ x = 10.

Answer: 10 shares.

Question — National percentage from state percentages

A national percentage cannot be found by simply averaging state percentages unless each state has the same number of schools. If Nᵢ is the number of schools in state i and pᵢ its percentage with playgrounds, the national percentage is (ΣNᵢpᵢ)/(ΣNᵢ). This requires the school counts and corresponding percentages.

One Minute Revision

  • Combined mean = (sum of group totals) ÷ (sum of group sizes).
  • Do not average group averages directly unless group sizes are equal.
  • For pooled sections, weight each depth by the section length.
  • The 100% stacked bar has equal total length for every category; segment lengths show shares.
  • When reading a graph, state that values are approximate if the graph is not labelled precisely.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Why is a weighted average needed?

Because a group with more observations contributes more to the combined total than a smaller group.

2. When can we simply average two means?

When the groups have the same size; otherwise weight by the number of observations.

3. What is the average formula for a mixture?

Total amount of the ingredient in all portions divided by the total mixture amount.
