Class 9 Mathematics – Chapter 5
I'm Up and Down, and Round and Round

Ganita Manjari – Part 1

This chapter develops the geometry of circles through construction, symmetry, congruence, Baudhāyana–Pythagoras reasoning, chord properties, arcs and cyclic quadrilaterals. The aim is to understand why the results are true, not simply memorise them.

🔑 Important Results

ResultWhat it tells us
CircumcircleThree non-collinear points determine one unique circle.
Equal chordsEqual chords subtend equal angles at the centre, and the converse also holds.
Perpendicular to a chordThe perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord.
Distance of chordsEqual chords are equidistant from the centre; the longer chord is nearer the centre.
Chord formula$L=2\sqrt{r^2-d^2}$, where $d$ is the perpendicular distance from the centre.
Arc angleThe angle at the centre is twice the angle subtended by the same arc at a point on the circle.
SemicircleAn angle subtended by a diameter at the circle is $90^\circ$.
Cyclic quadrilateralOpposite angles are supplementary: their sum is $180^\circ$.

📐 The 12 Theorems at a Glance

  1. A unique circle passes through three non-collinear points.
  2. Equal chords subtend equal angles at the centre.
  3. Chords subtending equal angles at the centre are equal.
  4. The line joining the centre to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to the chord.
  5. The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord.
  6. Equal chords are equidistant from the centre.
  7. Chords equidistant from the centre are equal.
  8. The longer of two unequal chords is nearer the centre.
  9. The angle subtended by an arc at the centre is twice the angle subtended at a point on the circle.
  10. If a segment subtends equal angles at two suitable points on the same side, the four points are concyclic.
  11. Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to $180^\circ$.
  12. If opposite angles of a quadrilateral sum to $180^\circ$, the quadrilateral is cyclic.

📌 Additional In-text Exercises

In the figure used for this activity, the measured angles are approximately $100^\circ$ and $205^\circ$, so the first is a minor arc and the second a major arc. Measurement can vary slightly with the printed figure and protractor use.

Page 113: A quadrilateral with angles $80^\circ,110^\circ,100^\circ,70^\circ$ can be cyclic because each pair of opposite angles sums to $180^\circ$.

🎯 How to Solve Numerical Questions from this Chapter

  1. Draw the circle and mark the centre.
  2. Drop a perpendicular from the centre to a chord whenever a chord length or distance is involved.
  3. Use the fact that this perpendicular bisects the chord.
  4. Apply Baudhāyana–Pythagoras to the resulting right triangle.
  5. For angle questions, identify whether the angle is central, inscribed, in a semicircle, or part of a cyclic quadrilateral.
  6. For proofs, write Given → To Prove → Construction/Reason → Conclusion.
