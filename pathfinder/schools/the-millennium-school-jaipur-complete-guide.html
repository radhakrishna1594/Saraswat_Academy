3. Learning approach and academics

The Millennium Schools describe their approach as student-centred and activity-based, with an emphasis on helping children understand concepts rather than relying only on rote learning. The group’s public material describes its Millennium Learning System and highlights intellectual, creative, physical and social development. The actual classroom experience, however, depends on implementation at the individual campus and in each class.

During a school visit, ask teachers to explain how the approach looks in an ordinary week—not only during an annual exhibition or special event.

  • Concept learning: How are Mathematics and Science taught—through demonstrations, practice, discussion, projects and regular feedback?
  • Reading and communication: What opportunities do students have for reading, speaking, writing and presenting their work?
  • Assessment: How frequently are classwork, homework, projects and tests assessed? How are parents informed when a child is struggling?
  • Individual support: Is remedial help available? How does the school support students who need extra time or additional challenge?
  • Senior classes: If the campus offers higher classes, ask which streams and subject combinations are currently available and verify them in writing.

4. Admissions: process and documents

Admission availability changes by class, campus and academic session. The school may ask parents to complete an enquiry or registration form, meet the admissions team, provide documents and attend a student interaction or assessment where applicable. Confirm the exact process for your child’s class rather than relying on a general school-chain description.

Documents to keep ready

  • Child’s birth certificate and recent passport-size photographs.
  • Child’s and parents’ identification documents, as requested by the school.
  • Previous class report card or progress report, where applicable.
  • Transfer certificate (TC), if required for the class and transfer circumstances.
  • Address proof and any medical or immunisation information requested by the school.
  • Any category, RTE or other documents applicable to the specific admission route.

Ask for the official age criteria for the entry class, the application deadline, whether an assessment or interaction is required, and the refund policy if admission is not completed. For RTE admissions, follow the current Rajasthan government notification and official process; do not assume that a school’s general admission process covers RTE seats.

7. Transport and daily routine

Transport is often a major part of a family’s school decision. The Jaipur listing mentions transport-related services, but the exact routes and costs need direct confirmation. Ask for the route map and estimated pickup/drop-off times for your neighbourhood before paying transport charges.

  • Is your exact locality covered for the coming session?
  • What is the expected pickup and drop-off time, and how much can it vary in traffic?
  • Who supervises children while boarding and leaving the bus?
  • How are parents notified about route delays, bus changes or emergencies?
  • What happens if a parent or authorised adult is not present at drop-off?
  • Are transport charges refundable if the route changes or the service is discontinued?

8. Results, progress and parent communication

When comparing schools, ask for campus-specific academic information rather than relying on broad claims about the school network. If you are considering Class X or XII, request the latest available board results for the exact campus, including the number of students who appeared, pass percentage, subject performance and the range of results. A list of top scorers alone does not show how the whole cohort performed.

For younger classes, ask how the school reports learning progress, attendance, behaviour and social-emotional development. Find out whom parents should contact when a child needs academic support and how quickly concerns are normally addressed.

9. Safety and wellbeing questions for parents

Safety should be evaluated through clear procedures, not just a brochure or a gate photograph. During the campus visit, ask to understand the school’s child-protection policy and how concerns are recorded, investigated and escalated.

  • Who is the designated child-safety or grievance contact?
  • What are the visitor-entry and student handover rules?
  • How does the school supervise corridors, washrooms, playgrounds and transport?
  • What first-aid and emergency response arrangements are in place?
  • How can a parent raise a concern confidentially, and who handles follow-up?
  • How does the school respond to bullying, exclusion or repeated classroom difficulties?

Parents can also ask to see current building-safety and fire-safety certificates or the school’s mandatory public disclosure page, where applicable.

10. Questions to ask before admission

  1. What is the exact campus address, and which classes are offered there this year?
  2. What is the campus’s current CBSE affiliation number and affiliation validity?
  3. Can I receive the complete fee circular and written details of optional charges?
  4. What is the class strength, and how many teachers or support staff are assigned?
  5. What is the homework policy and how are assessments communicated to parents?
  6. Which sports, clubs and creative activities are actually available at this campus?
  7. What are the transport routes, safety procedures and total transport charges?
  8. What is the process for academic support, counselling and parent complaints?
  9. Can I speak to the admissions team about the child’s particular class and subject needs?
  10. What is the withdrawal and fee-refund policy, and can I receive it in writing?
