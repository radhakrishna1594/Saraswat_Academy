Chapter 5: Combinatorics

Relations and functions are fundamental concepts in mathematics that describe the relationship between two sets of elements.

A relation is a set of ordered pairs, where each pair consists of an element from the first set and an element from the second set. A function is a special type of relation where each element in the first set (domain) is associated with exactly one element in the second set (range).

Functions can be represented in various ways, including:

  • Set notation: Using ordered pairs to represent the function.
  • Graphical representation: Plotting points on a coordinate plane to visualize the function.
  • Algebraic expression: Using equations to define the relationship between variables.

Understanding relations and functions is crucial for further studies in mathematics, as they form the basis for more advanced topics such as calculus and linear algebra.

EXERCISE 5.1

Question 1

A restaurant offers 4 starters, 5 main courses, and 3 desserts. In how many ways can a 3-course meal be ordered?

Choose one starter, one main course and one dessert. By the Fundamental Principle of Counting, the number of choices is 4 × 5 × 3 = 60.

Question 2

There are 5 doors to enter a hall and 3 different doors to exit. In how many ways can a person enter and exit the hall?

There are 5 choices for entering and 3 choices for leaving. Therefore, total ways = 5 × 3 = 15.

Question 3

A bicycle lock has 3 dials, each with digits 0 to 9. How many different lock combinations are possible if a digit can be repeated?

Each dial has 10 choices. Since repetition is allowed, total combinations = 10 × 10 × 10 = 103 = 1000.

Question 4

How many numbers between 2000 and 3000 can be formed from the digits 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 when repetition of digits is not allowed?

The thousands digit must be 2, so it has 1 choice. The remaining three positions are filled from the other 5 digits without repetition: 5 × 4 × 3.

Total = 1 × 5 × 4 × 3 = 60.

EXERCISE 5.2

Question 1

Find the HCF and LCM of 6! and 5!.

6! = 6 × 5!.

Hence 5! divides 6!. Therefore, HCF = 5! = 120 and LCM = 6! = 720.

Question 2

Find n if n!/(n − 3)! = 60.

n!/(n − 3)! = n(n − 1)(n − 2) = 60.

Since 5 × 4 × 3 = 60, n = 5.

Question 3

Find x, if 1/5! + 1/6! = x/7!.

Multiply by 7!:

x = 7!/5! + 7!/6! = (7 × 6) + 7 = 42 + 7 = 49.

Question 4

Find the value(s) of x, x ≥ 0:

(a) x! = 120. Since 5! = 120, x = 5.

(b) (x!)2 = 576. Thus x! = 24, so x = 4.

(c) (x!)2 − 25x! + 24 = 0. Put y = x!. Then y2 − 25y + 24 = 0 = (y − 24)(y − 1). Thus y = 24 or 1, giving x = 4 or x = 1.

(d) ((x + 2)! − (x + 1)!)/x! = 49. Factor (x + 1)!: numerator = (x + 1)![(x + 2) − 1] = (x + 1)!(x + 1). Dividing by x! gives (x + 1)2 = 49. Since x ≥ 0, x + 1 = 7, so x = 6.

EXERCISE 5.3

Question 1

In how many ways can 4 distinct cars be parked in 6 empty spaces?

Arrange 4 distinct cars in 6 available positions: 6P4 = 6 × 5 × 4 × 3 = 360.

Question 2

How many 3-letter words (with or without meaning) can be formed using the letters of the word “LOGIC”?

There are 5 distinct letters and order matters. Hence 5P3 = 5 × 4 × 3 = 60.

Question 3

A library has 5 distinct science books and 3 distinct math books. In how many ways can they be arranged on a shelf if the math books must occupy the first 3 positions?

The 3 distinct mathematics books can be arranged in the first three positions in 3! ways. The remaining 5 science books occupy the remaining positions in 5! ways.

Total = 3! × 5! = 6 × 120 = 720.

Question 4

In how many ways can 5 boys and 2 girls be seated in a row of 7 chairs if the 2 girls must always sit together?

Treat the two girls as one block. Then there are 6 units to arrange: 5 boys and 1 girls-block, in 6! ways. The two girls can be arranged within the block in 2! ways.

Total = 6! × 2! = 1440.

Question 5

How many 4-digit numbers can be formed using the digits 2, 4, 6, 8, 9 without repetition if the number must be strictly greater than 6000?

The thousands digit can be 6, 8 or 9: 3 choices. The remaining three places can be filled from the remaining four digits in 4 × 3 × 2 ways.

Total = 3 × 4 × 3 × 2 = 72.

Question 6

How many words can be formed from DAUGHTER so that (i) all vowels occur together, (ii) all vowels do not occur together?

DAUGHTER has 8 distinct letters and 3 vowels A, U, E.

(i) All vowels together: Treat AUE as one block. We then arrange 6 units, giving 6! ways, and arrange the 3 vowels within the block in 3! ways.

Thus total = 6! × 3! = 4320.

(ii) All vowels do not occur together: Total arrangements = 8! = 40320. Therefore, required = 40320 − 4320 = 36000.

EXERCISE 5.4

Question 1

In how many ways can 3 students be chosen from a class of 12 to represent the school?

Selection order does not matter. Therefore, 12C3 = (12 × 11 × 10)/(3 × 2 × 1) = 220.

Question 2

How many triangles can be formed from 12 points in a plane, of which 5 are collinear?

Total ways to choose 3 points from 12 = 12C3 = 220. The 5 collinear points produce 5C3 = 10 selections, none of which forms a triangle.

Therefore, number of triangles = 220 − 10 = 210.

Question 3

An examination paper contains 12 questions divided into two parts, A and B. Part A contains 7 questions and Part B contains 5 questions. A candidate must attempt 7 questions, selecting at least 3 from each part. In how many ways can the candidate select the questions?

Possible selections are (3 from A, 4 from B) or (4 from A, 3 from B).

Total = 7C35C4 + 7C45C3 = 35 × 5 + 35 × 10 = 525.

Question 4

How many diagonals does a polygon with 10 sides have?

Choose any two vertices: 10C2 = 45. Of these, 10 are sides. Hence diagonals = 45 − 10 = 35.

Question 5

If you invite 15 of your friends to a party and all shake hands exactly once, how many handshakes occur?

Each handshake corresponds to a pair of people. Thus the number is 15C2 = 15 × 14 / 2 = 105.

Question 6

A committee of 3 persons is to be constituted from a group of 2 men and 3 women. In how many ways can this be done? How many committees consist of 1 man and 2 women?

Total committees = 5C3 = 10.

For 1 man and 2 women: 2C1 × 3C2 = 2 × 3 = 6.
