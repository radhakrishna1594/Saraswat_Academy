Chapter Overview

Another important part of the chapter focuses on healthy lifestyle choices. Students explore how balanced nutrition, physical activity, hygiene and other healthy habits can help prevent many health problems. The chapter also discusses chronic and lifestyle-related diseases such as diabetes and the importance of prevention and management.

Overall, the chapter develops an understanding of how diseases affect individuals and communities and encourages students to make responsible, informed and healthy choices in everyday life.
Learn Clearly

Important Concepts and notes

1. Health

Health is more than simply being free from disease. It includes physical, mental and social well-being. Good health is supported by healthy habits, positive thinking, proper nutrition, physical activity, adequate sleep and healthy relationships.

2. Disease

A disease affects the normal functioning of the body or mind. Symptoms are what a person feels, such as pain or tiredness, while signs are changes that can be observed or measured, such as fever or a rash.

3. Communicable Diseases

Diseases caused by pathogens that can spread from one person to another are called communicable diseases. Examples include typhoid, dengue, flu, chickenpox and COVID-19.

4. How Communicable Diseases Spread

Pathogens can enter the body through the air, contaminated food or water and other routes. They may spread through coughing and sneezing, direct contact, or indirectly through shared personal items. Some pathogens are carried by insects such as mosquitoes and houseflies, which are called vectors.

5. Examples of Diseases and Their Spread

  • Tuberculosis (TB): Caused by bacteria and affects the lungs.
  • Hepatitis A: Caused by a virus and can spread through contaminated food or water.
  • Cholera: Caused by bacteria and commonly spreads through contaminated food or water.
  • Typhoid: Caused by bacteria and can spread through contaminated food or water.
  • Malaria: Caused by a protozoan and transmitted by mosquitoes.

The chapter emphasises hygiene, safe drinking water, properly cooked food, vaccination and other preventive measures for reducing the spread of diseases.

6. Non-Communicable Diseases

Non-communicable diseases (NCDs) are not caused by pathogens and do not spread from one person to another. They are often linked to lifestyle, diet and environmental factors.

Examples include diabetes, heart disease, cancer and asthma. Unhealthy eating habits, insufficient physical activity and other lifestyle factors can increase the risk of several NCDs.

7. Deficiency Diseases

Diseases caused by a lack of specific nutrients in the diet are called deficiency diseases. They are also non-communicable.

8. Immunity

Immunity is the natural ability of the body to fight diseases. The immune system protects the body against harmful pathogens.

9. Vaccination

Vaccines help protect against certain serious infections by training the immune system to recognise and fight harmful germs. Vaccines may contain weakened or dead pathogens, harmless parts of pathogens, or other components that help the immune system develop protection.

10. Antibiotics

Antibiotics are medicines used against bacterial infections. They work by targeting features of bacterial cells and therefore do not work against viral infections such as common cold and flu.

11. Prevention of Diseases

Prevention is important for both communicable and non-communicable diseases. Important preventive measures include maintaining personal hygiene, clean surroundings, safe food and water, vaccination, healthy eating, regular physical activity and adequate sleep.

12. Treatment and Management

Proper diagnosis and treatment are important for managing diseases. For non-communicable diseases, treatment may involve medicines, lifestyle changes and rehabilitation. Early diagnosis and continuous care can help control disease progression and prevent complications.

Examination Strategy

Exam Tips

  • Learn the definition of health and remember that it includes physical, mental and social well-being.
  • Clearly understand the difference between symptoms and signs, with suitable examples.
  • Remember the difference between communicable and non-communicable diseases.
  • Memorise important examples of communicable diseases such as typhoid, dengue, flu and chickenpox.
  • Remember that pathogens cause communicable diseases and can spread through different routes.
  • Understand the role of vectors, especially mosquitoes and houseflies, in spreading certain diseases.
  • Prepare examples of non-communicable diseases such as diabetes, heart disease, cancer and asthma.
  • Remember that non-communicable diseases are often associated with lifestyle, diet and environmental factors.
  • Understand immunity and the role of the immune system in protecting the body from harmful pathogens.
  • Learn how vaccines protect us by training the immune system to recognise and fight specific harmful germs.
  • Remember an important exam point: antibiotics work against bacterial infections, not viral infections.
  • For prevention-based questions, mention personal hygiene, clean surroundings, safe food and water, vaccination, healthy diet, exercise and adequate sleep.
  • For questions about disease management, remember the importance of early diagnosis, appropriate treatment and continuous care.

Exam Focus: Pay special attention to definitions, differences, examples, disease transmission, vectors, immunity, vaccination, antibiotics and methods of disease prevention.
