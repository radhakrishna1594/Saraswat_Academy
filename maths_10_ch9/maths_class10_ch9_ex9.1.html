Exercise 9.1

Question 1

A circus artist is climbing a 20 m long rope which is tightly stretched and tied from the top of a vertical pole to the ground. Find the height of the pole, if the angle made by the rope with the ground level is \(30^\circ\).

In the right-angled triangle, let \(AB\) be the height of the pole and \(AC\) be the rope.

Given:

$$AC=20\text{ m},\qquad \angle C=30^\circ$$

Using the sine ratio:

$$\sin30^\circ=\frac{AB}{AC}$$ $$\frac12=\frac{AB}{20}$$ $$AB=20\times\frac12=10\text{ m}$$

Therefore, the height of the pole is \(10\text{ m}\).

Question 2

A tree breaks due to a storm and the broken part bends so that the top of the tree touches the ground, making an angle of \(30^\circ\) with it. The distance between the foot of the tree and the point where the top touches the ground is 8 m. Find the height of the tree.

Let \(AB\) be the unbroken part of the tree and \(AC\) the broken part.

$$BC=8\text{ m},\qquad \angle C=30^\circ$$

For the unbroken part:

$$\tan30^\circ=\frac{AB}{BC}$$ $$\frac1{\sqrt3}=\frac{AB}{8}$$ $$AB=\frac8{\sqrt3}=\frac{8\sqrt3}{3}\text{ m}$$

For the broken part:

$$\sin30^\circ=\frac{AB}{AC}$$ $$\frac12=\frac{AB}{AC}$$ $$AC=2AB=\frac{16\sqrt3}{3}\text{ m}$$

Hence, the total height of the tree is:

$$AB+AC=\frac{8\sqrt3}{3}+\frac{16\sqrt3}{3} =\frac{24\sqrt3}{3}=8\sqrt3\text{ m}$$

Therefore, the height of the tree is \(8\sqrt3\text{ m}\approx13.86\text{ m}\).

Question 3

A contractor plans to install two slides for children. Find the length of each slide.

(i) Slide for children below 5 years

Height \(=1.5\text{ m}\), angle with the ground \(=30^\circ\).

Let the length of the slide be \(L\).

$$\sin30^\circ=\frac{1.5}{L}$$ $$\frac12=\frac{1.5}{L}$$ $$L=3\text{ m}$$

(ii) Slide for elder children

Height \(=3\text{ m}\), angle with the ground \(=60^\circ\).

$$\sin60^\circ=\frac3L$$ $$\frac{\sqrt3}{2}=\frac3L$$ $$L=\frac6{\sqrt3}=2\sqrt3\text{ m}$$ $$L\approx3.46\text{ m}$$

Lengths of the slides are \(3\text{ m}\) and \(2\sqrt3\text{ m}\approx3.46\text{ m}\).

Question 4

The angle of elevation of the top of a tower from a point on the ground, which is 30 m away from the foot of the tower, is \(30^\circ\). Find the height of the tower.

Let the height of the tower be \(h\).

$$\tan30^\circ=\frac{h}{30}$$ $$\frac1{\sqrt3}=\frac{h}{30}$$ $$h=\frac{30}{\sqrt3}=10\sqrt3\text{ m}$$ $$h\approx17.32\text{ m}$$

Therefore, the height of the tower is \(10\sqrt3\text{ m}\).

Question 5

A kite is flying at a height of 60 m above the ground. The string attached to the kite makes an angle of \(60^\circ\) with the ground. Find the length of the string.

Let the length of the string be \(L\).

$$\sin60^\circ=\frac{60}{L}$$ $$\frac{\sqrt3}{2}=\frac{60}{L}$$ $$L=\frac{120}{\sqrt3}=40\sqrt3\text{ m}$$ $$L\approx69.28\text{ m}$$

Therefore, the length of the string is \(40\sqrt3\text{ m}\).

Question 6

A 1.5 m tall boy is standing at some distance from a 30 m tall building. The angle of elevation from his eyes to the top of the building increases from \(30^\circ\) to \(60^\circ\) as he walks towards the building. Find the distance he walked.

The vertical height between the boy's eyes and the top of the building is:

$$30-1.5=28.5\text{ m}$$

Let the initial distance be \(x\) m.

$$\tan30^\circ=\frac{28.5}{x}$$ $$\frac1{\sqrt3}=\frac{28.5}{x}$$ $$x=28.5\sqrt3\text{ m}$$

Let the final distance be \(y\) m.

$$\tan60^\circ=\frac{28.5}{y}$$ $$\sqrt3=\frac{28.5}{y}$$ $$y=\frac{28.5}{\sqrt3}=9.5\sqrt3\text{ m}$$

Therefore, the distance walked is:

$$x-y=28.5\sqrt3-9.5\sqrt3=19\sqrt3\text{ m}$$ $$19\sqrt3\approx32.91\text{ m}$$

Therefore, the boy walked \(19\sqrt3\text{ m}\).

Question 7

From a point on the ground, the angles of elevation of the bottom and the top of a transmission tower fixed at the top of a 20 m high building are \(45^\circ\) and \(60^\circ\), respectively. Find the height of the tower.

Let the height of the tower be \(h\) m and the distance from the observation point to the building be \(x\) m.

For the bottom of the tower:

$$\tan45^\circ=\frac{20}{x}$$ $$1=\frac{20}{x}\quad\Rightarrow\quad x=20\text{ m}$$

For the top of the tower:

$$\tan60^\circ=\frac{20+h}{20}$$ $$\sqrt3=\frac{20+h}{20}$$ $$20\sqrt3=20+h$$ $$h=20(\sqrt3-1)\text{ m}$$ $$h\approx14.64\text{ m}$$

Therefore, the height of the transmission tower is \(20(\sqrt3-1)\text{ m}\).

Question 8

A statue, 1.6 m tall, stands on the top of a pedestal. From a point on the ground, the angle of elevation of the top of the statue is \(60^\circ\) and from the same point the angle of elevation of the top of the pedestal is \(45^\circ\). Find the height of the pedestal.

Let the height of the pedestal be \(h\) m and the horizontal distance from the observation point be \(x\) m.

For the top of the pedestal:

$$\tan45^\circ=\frac{h}{x}$$ $$1=\frac{h}{x}\quad\Rightarrow\quad x=h$$

For the top of the statue:

$$\tan60^\circ=\frac{h+1.6}{x}$$ $$\sqrt3=\frac{h+1.6}{h}$$ $$\sqrt3h=h+1.6$$ $$h(\sqrt3-1)=1.6$$ $$h=\frac{1.6}{\sqrt3-1}$$ $$h=\frac{1.6(\sqrt3+1)}{2}=0.8(\sqrt3+1)\text{ m}$$ $$h\approx2.19\text{ m}$$

Therefore, the height of the pedestal is \(0.8(\sqrt3+1)\text{ m}\approx2.19\text{ m}\).

Question 9

The angle of elevation of the top of a building from the foot of a tower is \(30^\circ\) and the angle of elevation of the top of the tower from the foot of the building is \(60^\circ\). If the tower is 50 m high, find the height of the building.

Let the height of the building be \(h\) m and the distance between the two structures be \(x\) m.

From the foot of the building:

$$\tan60^\circ=\frac{50}{x}$$ $$\sqrt3=\frac{50}{x}\quad\Rightarrow\quad x=\frac{50}{\sqrt3}$$

From the foot of the tower:

$$\tan30^\circ=\frac{h}{x}$$ $$\frac1{\sqrt3}=\frac{h}{50/\sqrt3}$$ $$h=\frac{50}{3}\text{ m}$$ $$h\approx16.67\text{ m}$$

Therefore, the height of the building is \(\frac{50}{3}\text{ m}\approx16.67\text{ m}\).

Question 10

Two poles of equal heights are standing opposite each other on either side of a road, which is 80 m wide. From a point between them on the road, the angles of elevation of the tops of the poles are \(60^\circ\) and \(30^\circ\), respectively. Find the height of the poles and the distances of the point from the poles.

Let the common height be \(h\) m and the distances from the point be \(x\) m and \((80-x)\) m.

For the first pole:

$$\tan60^\circ=\frac{h}{x}$$ $$\sqrt3=\frac{h}{x}\quad\Rightarrow\quad h=x\sqrt3$$

For the second pole:

$$\tan30^\circ=\frac{h}{80-x}$$ $$\frac1{\sqrt3}=\frac{h}{80-x}\quad\Rightarrow\quad h=\frac{80-x}{\sqrt3}$$

Since the poles have equal heights:

$$x\sqrt3=\frac{80-x}{\sqrt3}$$ $$3x=80-x$$ $$4x=80\quad\Rightarrow\quad x=20\text{ m}$$

The other distance is:

$$80-20=60\text{ m}$$

Hence the height is:

$$h=20\sqrt3\text{ m}\approx34.64\text{ m}$$

Therefore, the height of each pole is \(20\sqrt3\text{ m}\), and the distances are \(20\text{ m}\) and \(60\text{ m}\).

Question 11

A TV tower stands vertically on a bank of a canal. From a point on the other bank directly opposite the tower, the angle of elevation of the top of the tower is \(60^\circ\). From another point 20 m away from this point on the line joining this point to the foot of the tower, the angle of elevation of the top of the tower is \(30^\circ\). Find the height of the tower and the width of the canal.

Let the width of the canal be \(x\) m and the height of the tower be \(h\) m.

From the point directly opposite the tower:

$$\tan60^\circ=\frac{h}{x}$$ $$\sqrt3=\frac{h}{x}\quad\Rightarrow\quad h=x\sqrt3$$

The second point is 20 m farther from the tower, so its distance is \(x+20\) m.

$$\tan30^\circ=\frac{h}{x+20}$$ $$\frac1{\sqrt3}=\frac{h}{x+20}$$ $$h=\frac{x+20}{\sqrt3}$$

Equating the two expressions for \(h\):

$$x\sqrt3=\frac{x+20}{\sqrt3}$$ $$3x=x+20$$ $$2x=20\quad\Rightarrow\quad x=10\text{ m}$$

Therefore:

$$h=10\sqrt3\text{ m}\approx17.32\text{ m}$$

Therefore, the width of the canal is \(10\text{ m}\) and the height of the tower is \(10\sqrt3\text{ m}\).

Question 12

From the top of a 7 m high building, the angle of elevation of the top of a cable tower is \(60^\circ\) and the angle of depression of its foot is \(45^\circ\). Determine the height of the tower.

Let the horizontal distance between the building and the tower be \(x\) m.

Using the angle of depression:

$$\tan45^\circ=\frac7x$$ $$1=\frac7x\quad\Rightarrow\quad x=7\text{ m}$$

Let the height of the tower be \(h\) m. The vertical difference between the top of the building and the top of the tower is \(h-7\).

$$\tan60^\circ=\frac{h-7}{7}$$ $$\sqrt3=\frac{h-7}{7}$$ $$h-7=7\sqrt3$$ $$h=7(\sqrt3+1)\text{ m}$$ $$h\approx19.12\text{ m}$$

Therefore, the height of the tower is \(7(\sqrt3+1)\text{ m}\).

Question 13

As observed from the top of a 75 m high lighthouse from sea-level, the angles of depression of two ships are \(30^\circ\) and \(45^\circ\). If one ship is exactly behind the other on the same side of the lighthouse, find the distance between the two ships.

For the nearer ship, the angle of depression is \(45^\circ\). Let its horizontal distance from the lighthouse be \(x\) m.

$$\tan45^\circ=\frac{75}{x}$$ $$1=\frac{75}{x}\quad\Rightarrow\quad x=75\text{ m}$$

For the farther ship, the angle of depression is \(30^\circ\). Let its horizontal distance be \(y\) m.

$$\tan30^\circ=\frac{75}{y}$$ $$\frac1{\sqrt3}=\frac{75}{y}\quad\Rightarrow\quad y=75\sqrt3\text{ m}$$

Since the ships are on the same straight line from the lighthouse:

$$\text{Distance between ships}=y-x$$ $$=75\sqrt3-75=75(\sqrt3-1)\text{ m}$$ $$\approx54.90\text{ m}$$

Therefore, the distance between the two ships is \(75(\sqrt3-1)\text{ m}\).

Question 14

A 1.2 m tall girl spots a balloon moving with the wind in a horizontal line at a height of 88.2 m from the ground. The angle of elevation of the balloon from the eyes of the girl is \(60^\circ\). After some time, the angle of elevation reduces to \(30^\circ\). Find the distance travelled by the balloon during the interval.

The height of the balloon above the girl's eyes is:

$$88.2-1.2=87\text{ m}$$

Let the initial horizontal distance be \(x\) m.

$$\tan60^\circ=\frac{87}{x}$$ $$\sqrt3=\frac{87}{x}\quad\Rightarrow\quad x=\frac{87}{\sqrt3}=29\sqrt3\text{ m}$$

Let the final horizontal distance be \(y\) m.

$$\tan30^\circ=\frac{87}{y}$$ $$\frac1{\sqrt3}=\frac{87}{y}\quad\Rightarrow\quad y=87\sqrt3\text{ m}$$

The balloon moves horizontally, so the distance travelled is:

$$y-x=87\sqrt3-29\sqrt3=58\sqrt3\text{ m}$$ $$58\sqrt3\approx100.46\text{ m}$$

Therefore, the balloon travelled \(58\sqrt3\text{ m}\approx100.46\text{ m}\).

Question 15

A straight highway leads to the foot of a tower. A man standing at the top of the tower observes a car at an angle of depression of \(30^\circ\), which is approaching the foot of the tower with a uniform speed. Six seconds later the angle of depression of the car is \(60^\circ\). Find the time taken by the car to reach the foot of the tower from this point.

Let the height of the tower be \(h\) m. Let the initial distance of the car from the foot of the tower be \(x\) m and its distance after 6 seconds be \(y\) m.

At the first position:

$$\tan30^\circ=\frac{h}{x}$$ $$\frac1{\sqrt3}=\frac{h}{x}\quad\Rightarrow\quad x=h\sqrt3$$

After 6 seconds:

$$\tan60^\circ=\frac{h}{y}$$ $$\sqrt3=\frac{h}{y}\quad\Rightarrow\quad y=\frac{h}{\sqrt3}$$

Distance travelled by the car in 6 seconds:

$$x-y=h\sqrt3-\frac{h}{\sqrt3}$$ $$=\frac{3h-h}{\sqrt3}=\frac{2h}{\sqrt3}$$

Hence, the speed of the car is:

$$v=\frac{\frac{2h}{\sqrt3}}6=\frac{h}{3\sqrt3}\text{ m/s}$$

From the second position, the remaining distance is \(y=\frac{h}{\sqrt3}\). Therefore:

$$t=\frac{\text{distance}}{\text{speed}} =\frac{h/\sqrt3}{h/(3\sqrt3)} =3\text{ s}$$

Therefore, the car will take \(3\) seconds to reach the foot of the tower.
