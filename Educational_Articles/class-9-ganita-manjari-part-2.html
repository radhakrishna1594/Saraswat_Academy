What Is Ganita Manjari Part 2?

Ganita Manjari Part 2 is the second part of the latest NCERT Mathematics textbook for Class 9. It continues the redesigned Class 9 Mathematics course for the 2026–27 academic session.

Part 1 contains Chapters 1 to 8, while Part 2 continues with Chapters 9 to 14. Together, the two parts provide the complete Class 9 Mathematics textbook for the new course structure.

The new book places greater emphasis on mathematical reasoning, connections between ideas, interpretation, problem solving and applying mathematics to situations rather than treating mathematics only as a collection of formulas.

Important: Students should always match their school timetable and assessment plan with the latest instructions issued by their school, CBSE and NCERT. Textbook availability, school sequencing and assessment schedules can vary.

Complete Chapter-wise Overview

Chapter 9

Propositions and their Converses

This chapter introduces students to mathematical statements that can be judged as true or false and develops the idea of reasoning with conditional statements.

  • Understanding propositions and their truth value.
  • Conditional statements and logical structure.
  • Understanding a converse and how it differs from the original statement.
  • Using examples, counterexamples and reasoning to test statements.
  • Building the habit of explaining why a mathematical statement is true.
Chapter 10

How Quantities Combine: Understanding Data

This chapter develops statistical thinking and helps students interpret data instead of simply calculating individual values.

  • Organising and interpreting data.
  • Combining quantities and understanding representative values.
  • Reading and comparing graphical representations.
  • Understanding how different groups of observations can be compared.
  • Using mathematical reasoning while interpreting real-world information.
Chapter 11

The World of Algorithms

This chapter introduces algorithmic thinking: breaking a problem into a clear sequence of logical steps that can be followed and checked.

  • What an algorithm is and why algorithms are useful.
  • Writing systematic steps for solving problems.
  • Recognising patterns and repeated procedures.
  • Checking whether a sequence of steps produces the intended result.
  • Developing logical and computational thinking through mathematics.
Chapter 12

Quadrilaterals

This geometry chapter studies four-sided figures and develops understanding through properties, relationships and reasoning.

  • Properties and classification of quadrilaterals.
  • Parallelograms and their important properties.
  • Relationships among sides, angles and diagonals.
  • Mid-point based results and geometric reasoning.
  • Using propositions, converses and proofs in geometry.
Chapter 13

Two Variables, One Line

This chapter develops the connection between two-variable linear relationships, equations and their graphical representation.

  • Understanding linear equations involving two variables.
  • Finding and interpreting solutions.
  • Representing solutions on a coordinate plane.
  • Understanding the relationship between an equation and a straight line.
  • Using graphs to interpret mathematical relationships.
Chapter 14

Math of Space: Surface Area and Volume

The final chapter connects geometry with measurement of three-dimensional objects and develops practical understanding of surface area and volume.

  • Understanding three-dimensional shapes and their measurements.
  • Surface area of common solids.
  • Volume and its units.
  • Applying formulas to real-life measurement problems.
  • Checking units and interpreting answers sensibly.

What Makes Part 2 Different?

Students coming from the older Class 9 Mathematics textbook may notice that the new Ganita Manjari structure uses different chapter names and a different organisation of concepts. The emphasis is not simply on memorising a traditional list of formulas.

  • Reasoning: Students are encouraged to justify statements and examine whether conclusions follow logically.
  • Application: Mathematical ideas are connected with situations, interpretation and problem solving.
  • Representation: Data, equations, graphs and geometric figures are used together to understand concepts.
  • Algorithmic thinking: Students are introduced to systematic procedures and step-by-step problem solving.
  • Concept connections: Logic, algebra, geometry, statistics and mensuration are treated as connected mathematical ideas.

How to Study Ganita Manjari Part 2 Effectively

1. Read the concept before memorising the formula

Start by understanding the situation, definition, diagram or example given in the textbook. Once the meaning is clear, formulas become easier to remember and apply.

2. Maintain a mathematics notebook

Keep separate space for definitions, important results, diagrams, solved examples, mistakes and questions that you want to revisit.

3. Practise reasoning questions in writing

For geometry and propositions, do not write only the final answer. Practise explaining the reason for every important step.

4. Draw graphs and figures yourself

For quadrilaterals and two-variable relationships, drawing the figure or graph is an important part of understanding the mathematics.

5. Check units in mensuration

Area and volume use different units. Always write the unit with the final answer and check whether the unit matches the quantity being calculated.

6. Practise without looking at the solution

After studying an example, close the book and attempt a similar problem independently. This reveals whether you actually understood the method.

Common Mistakes Students Should Avoid

  1. Memorising a theorem or result without understanding its conditions.
  2. Confusing a statement with its converse.
  3. Reading data but not checking the scale or units used in a graph.
  4. Skipping diagrams in geometry problems.
  5. Writing an equation without interpreting what its variables represent.
  6. Using the surface-area formula when the question asks for volume, or vice versa.
  7. Leaving units out of mensuration answers.
  8. Copying a solved example without attempting a similar problem independently.

How Part 2 Fits with Part 1

Ganita Manjari Part 1 and Part 2 should be viewed as parts of the same Class 9 Mathematics course. Part 1 establishes foundations in areas such as coordinates, linear polynomials, numbers, algebraic identities, circles, perimeter and area, probability, and sequences and progressions.

Part 2 then extends mathematical thinking into propositions and reasoning, data, algorithms, quadrilaterals, two-variable linear relationships, and three-dimensional measurement.

This means students should not treat Part 2 as an isolated book. Concepts learned earlier can support later work, especially in algebra, coordinate representation, geometry and mathematical reasoning.

Ganita Manjari Part 2 for Exam Preparation

For school examinations, students should use the prescribed textbook as the primary source and follow the exercises and assessment guidance provided by their teacher and school.

  • Complete textbook examples before moving to additional questions.
  • Practise all exercise questions assigned by your school.
  • Create a chapter-wise formula and result sheet.
  • Practise diagrams and graphs repeatedly rather than only reading them.
  • Take timed mixed-chapter tests after completing the syllabus.
  • Review incorrect questions and maintain a mistake notebook.
