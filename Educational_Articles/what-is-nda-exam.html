NDA Exam 2026: Complete Guide to Syllabus, Dates, Eligibility & Preparation


The National Defence Academy (NDA) Exam is one of India's most prestigious competitive examinations conducted by the Union Public Service Commission (UPSC). It provides an opportunity for young aspirants to begin their journey towards becoming commissioned officers in the Indian Army, Navy and Air Force.

If you are a Class 11 or Class 12 student dreaming of wearing the uniform and serving the nation, understanding the NDA syllabus, eligibility criteria, examination pattern and preparation strategy is extremely important.

This complete guide by Saraswat Academy explains the NDA examination in simple language and covers important information about the exam, eligibility, syllabus, selection process and preparation strategy.


What is the NDA Exam?

The National Defence Academy (NDA) and Naval Academy (NA) Examination is conducted by UPSC for admission to the Army, Navy and Air Force wings of the National Defence Academy and the 10+2 Cadet Entry Scheme of the Indian Naval Academy.

The National Defence Academy is located at Khadakwasla, Pune. It is a tri-service training academy where cadets of the Army, Navy and Air Force train together before proceeding to their respective service academies for further training.

NDA is conducted twice every year. The selection process involves a written examination followed by the Services Selection Board (SSB) assessment and medical fitness requirements.

NDA is not just an academic examination. It tests knowledge, reasoning ability, communication skills, awareness, confidence and the qualities required for a career in the Armed Forces.


Why is the NDA Exam Important?

For students who want to join the Armed Forces soon after Class 12, NDA provides one of the most direct and prestigious pathways. It combines academic education, military training, physical development and leadership development.

NDA Exam Pattern

The NDA written examination consists of two objective-type papers: Mathematics and the General Ability Test (GAT). Each paper is conducted for 2 hours and 30 minutes.

Paper Subject Duration Maximum Marks
Paper I Mathematics 2 Hours 30 Minutes 300
Paper II General Ability Test (GAT) 2 Hours 30 Minutes 600
Total Written Examination 900

The written examination is followed by the SSB Test/Interview, which carries another 900 marks.

The Mathematics paper carries 300 marks. It tests mathematical understanding, numerical ability and problem-solving skills.

NDA Eligibility Criteria

Eligibility is one of the most important things an aspirant should check before beginning preparation. UPSC specifies the exact age and educational requirements in every examination notification.

Eligibility Factor Requirement
Nationality Candidates must satisfy the nationality conditions specified by UPSC.
Gender Eligible unmarried male and female candidates can apply.
Marital Status Candidates must be unmarried as prescribed in the notification.
Age Generally around 16½ to 19½ years, with the exact date-of-birth window specified separately in each notification.
Army Wing Class 12 passed or appearing from a recognised board.
Air Force & Naval Wings Class 12 passed or appearing with Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics.

Note: The exact age window and other conditions must be checked against the notification for the particular NDA examination you intend to take.


Can Arts Students Apply for NDA?

Yes. Students from the Arts stream can apply for the Army Wing provided they satisfy the other eligibility requirements.

However, candidates who want to apply for the Air Force or Naval Wings need to have studied the required science subjects, including Physics and Mathematics, at the Class 12 level.

Stream Army Wing Air Force / Navy
Science with PCM Yes Yes
Commerce Yes No, if required Physics & Mathematics are not studied
Arts Yes No, if required Physics & Mathematics are not studied

Can Girls Give the NDA Exam?

Yes. Eligible unmarried female candidates can apply for the NDA examination. Female candidates are included in the NDA selection process subject to the applicable eligibility, vacancy, medical and physical standards.

The inclusion of female candidates has opened the NDA pathway to more young aspirants who wish to pursue a career as officers in India's Armed Forces.


NDA Selection Process

Clearing the written examination is only one stage of the NDA selection process. Candidates must successfully clear subsequent stages.

  1. UPSC Written Examination
  2. Services Selection Board (SSB) Assessment
  3. Medical Examination
  4. Final Selection / Merit
  5. Training

The SSB process evaluates a candidate's personality, leadership potential, communication, decision-making ability, teamwork and other officer-like qualities.


How to Prepare for the NDA Exam?

NDA preparation should ideally begin well before the examination. A student should develop a balanced routine covering academics, current affairs, English, mathematics, physical fitness and personality development.

1. Build Strong Mathematical Fundamentals

Mathematics carries 300 marks, so candidates should first strengthen their fundamentals. Begin with concepts and then gradually move towards timed practice.

2. Follow the NDA Syllabus

Do not prepare randomly. Divide the syllabus into manageable topics and maintain a checklist of completed, pending and weak areas.

3. Practise Previous Year Questions

Previous year papers help candidates understand the nature of questions, difficulty level, recurring concepts and time pressure.

4. Improve English Every Day

Read English newspapers, editorials and quality articles. Learn new vocabulary and practise grammar questions regularly.

5. Develop Current Affairs Awareness

Make current affairs a daily habit. Focus particularly on national and international events, defence developments, science and technology, sports, geography and important government initiatives.

6. Take Mock Tests

Mock tests are important for developing examination temperament. After every mock test, analyse mistakes rather than simply checking the score.

7. Work on Physical Fitness

NDA aspirants should not wait until the written examination is over to start taking physical fitness seriously. A disciplined routine involving running, basic strength exercises, flexibility and healthy habits can help build physical readiness.

8. Develop Confidence and Communication Skills

SSB preparation should not be treated as memorising interview answers. Candidates should develop genuine communication, awareness, teamwork, confidence and decision-making abilities.

Is the NDA Exam Tough?

The NDA examination is certainly competitive, but it is not impossible. The difficulty comes from the combination of a broad syllabus, objective questions, time pressure, negative marking and the subsequent SSB selection process.

A student who starts preparation early, understands concepts, practises regularly, follows current affairs and works consistently on personality and fitness can build a strong foundation for NDA.

The most important qualities are discipline, consistency, accuracy, self-confidence and willingness to improve.
