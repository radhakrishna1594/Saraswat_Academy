Nouns in English Grammar: Types and Examples

Look around the room. You may see a desk, a bag, a window and a book. You may also think about your school, your family or a feeling such as happiness. Words that name people, places, things, animals or ideas are called nouns.

1. What is a noun?

A noun is a naming word. It can name a person, place, animal, thing or idea.

What does it name?Examples
Personteacher, doctor, Riya
Placeschool, Jaipur, park
Animaldog, elephant, rabbit
Thingpencil, phone, chair
Idea or feelinghonesty, joy, courage

Example: Riya took her book to school.

Nouns: Riya, book, school.

2. Common nouns and proper nouns

A common noun names a person, place or thing in a general way. A proper noun gives its particular name and usually begins with a capital letter.

Common nounProper noun
girlAnanya
cityJaipur
countryIndia
riverGanga
monthOctober

Remember: Write proper nouns with capital letters: “We live in India,” not “We live in india.”

3. Collective nouns

A collective noun names a group of people, animals or things as one unit.

Collective nounExample
teamOur team won the match.
flockA flock of birds flew past.
herdA herd of elephants crossed the road.
classThe class listened carefully.
bunchShe bought a bunch of grapes.

The group word helps us talk about several members together.

4. Abstract nouns

An abstract noun names a feeling, quality, state or idea that we cannot usually touch or see as a physical object.

Abstract nounExample sentence
honestyHonesty builds trust.
kindnessHer kindness made us happy.
braveryThe firefighter showed bravery.
childhoodI remember my childhood fondly.
happinessHis happiness was clear.

We cannot hold kindness in our hands, but we can recognise it through people's actions.

5. Concrete nouns

A concrete noun names something we can experience through our senses—for example, something we can see, hear, touch, taste or smell.

Examples: mango, bell, flower, rain, perfume and stone.

Compare flower (a concrete noun) with beauty (an abstract noun). One names a physical thing; the other names a quality or idea.

6. Countable and uncountable nouns

Countable nouns can be counted as individual items: one pencil, two pencils. Uncountable nouns are usually treated as a mass or whole rather than separate items: water, rice or information.

Countable nounsUncountable nouns
one apple, three appleswater
a chair, four chairsrice
two ideasinformation
many booksadvice
a student, several studentsfurniture

Use many with countable plural nouns and much with uncountable nouns in ordinary quantity questions.

Correct: “How many books do you have?”

Correct: “How much water is left?”

Remember: we normally say “some advice” or “a piece of advice”, not “an advice”.

7. Singular and plural nouns

A singular noun refers to one person or thing. A plural noun refers to more than one.

SingularPlural
bookbooks
classclasses
babybabies
leafleaves
childchildren
toothteeth
sheepsheep

Most nouns add -s or -es, but some change spelling and a few stay the same. Learn irregular forms as you meet them.

8. Common mistakes to avoid

IncorrectCorrectWhy?
an advicea piece of adviceAdvice is usually uncountable.
many furnituremuch furniture / many pieces of furnitureFurniture is usually uncountable.
two childstwo childrenChild has an irregular plural.
i live in indiaI live in India.Capitalise I and proper nouns.

9. Practice questions

  4. Is honesty concrete or abstract?
  5. Is rice usually countable or uncountable?
  6. Write the plural of baby.
  7. Choose the correct word: How (many/much) water do you need?
  8. Correct this sentence: “She gave me an advice.”
