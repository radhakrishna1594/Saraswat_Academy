Types of Sentences in English Grammar

Every sentence has a purpose. We may use a sentence to give information, ask a question, give an instruction or express a strong feeling. Learn the four main types of sentences and how to punctuate them correctly.

1. What is a sentence?

A sentence is a group of words that expresses a complete thought. It usually begins with a capital letter and ends with suitable punctuation.

  • The children are playing. — a complete statement.
  • Are the children playing? — a complete question.

A group of words such as because the children does not usually express a complete thought on its own.

2. The four main types of sentences

3. Declarative or assertive sentences

A declarative sentence makes a statement. It may tell us something, describe a situation or express an opinion.

  • Jaipur is the capital of Rajasthan.
  • My sister enjoys reading.
  • We did not miss the bus.
  • Learning grammar takes practice.

Both positive and negative statements can be declarative. They usually end with a full stop.

4. Interrogative sentences

An interrogative sentence asks a question and usually ends with a question mark.

Yes/no questions

  • Are you ready?
  • Did she finish her homework?
  • Will they visit tomorrow?

Wh- questions

These often begin with words such as who, what, where, when, why or how.

  • Where do you live?
  • Why is the child laughing?
  • How did you solve the problem?

Use a question mark for a direct question. An indirect question usually takes a full stop: She asked where I lived.

5. Imperative sentences

An imperative sentence gives a command, instruction, request, warning or advice. The subject you is usually understood rather than stated.

PurposeExample
CommandStand in a line.
InstructionTurn left at the crossing.
RequestPlease lend me your pen.
AdviceDrink enough water.
WarningLook out!
Negative instructionDo not touch the wire.

Imperative sentences may end with a full stop or, when the feeling is strong, an exclamation mark.

6. Exclamatory sentences

An exclamatory sentence expresses a strong feeling such as joy, surprise, sadness, admiration or anger. It ends with an exclamation mark.

  • What a wonderful performance!
  • How quickly the time passed!
  • That was an amazing goal!
  • What a shame!

Do not use an exclamation mark after every sentence. Save it for genuine emphasis or strong feeling.

7. Compare the sentence types

The same basic idea can be expressed in different ways depending on the speaker's purpose.

TypeExample about the window
DeclarativeThe window is open.
InterrogativeIs the window open?
ImperativePlease close the window.
ExclamatoryHow cold it is near this open window!

Classify a sentence by what it is doing in context, not just by one word. For example, an imperative can be polite, and a question can sometimes function as a request.

10. Quick revision

  • Declarative: gives information or states an idea; usually ends with a full stop.
  • Interrogative: asks a direct question; ends with a question mark.
  • Imperative: gives a command, request, instruction or advice; the subject “you” is usually understood.
  • Exclamatory: expresses strong feeling; ends with an exclamation mark.
  • Start every sentence with a capital letter and choose punctuation that matches its purpose.
