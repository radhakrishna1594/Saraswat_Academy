Affirmative and Negative Sentences

Affirmative sentences tell us that something is true or happens. Negative sentences tell us that something is not true or does not happen. Learn how to form both types correctly in different tenses.

1. What are affirmative and negative sentences?

An affirmative sentence states that something is so, happens or exists. A negative sentence expresses that something is not so, does not happen or does not exist.

AffirmativeNegative
Riya likes mangoes.Riya does not like mangoes.
They are ready.They are not ready.
We visited the museum.We did not visit the museum.
He can swim.He cannot swim.

A negative sentence commonly uses not or a negative word such as never, nobody or nothing.

2. Negative sentences with be verbs

When a sentence uses a form of be (am, is, are, was, were), place not after the verb.

AffirmativeNegative
I am busy.I am not busy.
She is at home.She is not at home.
They are hungry.They are not hungry.
He was late.He was not late.
We were tired.We were not tired.

Common contractions are isn't, aren't, wasn't and weren't. In standard English, say I'm not, rather than using “amn't”.

3. Negative sentences in the simple present

With most main verbs in the simple present, use do not (don't) or does not (doesn't) before the base form of the verb.

SubjectPatternExample
I / you / we / theySubject + do not + V1They do not play chess.
He / she / itSubject + does not + V1She does not play chess.

Remember: after doesn't, use the base form. Say He doesn't work, not He doesn't works.

4. Negative sentences in the simple past

For most main verbs in the simple past, use did not (didn't) + V1. The past meaning is carried by did, so the main verb returns to its base form.

AffirmativeNegative
I watched the match.I did not watch the match.
She went to school.She did not go to school.
They bought a car.They did not buy a car.

With the past forms of be, use was not or were not: He was not ill. / We were not late.

5. Negative sentences in continuous tenses

In continuous tenses, put not after the form of be and before the verb ending in -ing.

TenseAffirmativeNegative
Present continuousShe is reading.She is not reading.
Past continuousThey were playing.They were not playing.
Future continuousI will be waiting.I will not be waiting.

6. Negative sentences with perfect tenses

In perfect tenses, place not after have, has or had.

TenseAffirmativeNegative
Present perfectHe has finished.He has not finished.
Past perfectWe had arrived.We had not arrived.

With future forms using will, put not after will: They will not attend.

7. Negative sentences with modal verbs

Modal verbs include can, could, may, might, must, should and would. Put not directly after the modal verb.

AffirmativeNegative
I can drive.I cannot (can't) drive.
You should worry.You should not (shouldn't) worry.
She will agree.She will not (won't) agree.
We must leave.We must not (mustn't) leave.

Notice that the main verb stays in its base form after a modal: cannot swim, not cannot swims.

8. Other negative words

English has negative words that can make a sentence negative without adding not to the verb.

  • Never: I never skip breakfast.
  • Nobody: Nobody answered the phone.
  • Nothing: There is nothing in the box.
  • Nowhere: We had nowhere to sit.

Avoid an unintended double negative in standard English. For example, use I didn't see anybody or I saw nobody, rather than I didn't see nobody.

9. Common mistakes

IncorrectCorrectReason
She don't like tea.She doesn't like tea.Use does not with she/he/it.
He doesn't plays cricket.He doesn't play cricket.Use V1 after doesn't.
They didn't went home.They didn't go home.Use V1 after didn't.
I not am ready.I am not ready.Put not after the be verb.
She cannot to swim.She cannot swim.Do not use “to” after a modal verb.
I don't know nothing.I don't know anything.Avoid unintended double negatives.

11. Quick revision

  • Affirmative sentences state that something is so; negative sentences express the opposite or absence.
  • With am/is/are/was/were, place not after the verb.
  • In the simple present, use don't/doesn't + V1 for most main verbs.
  • In the simple past, use didn't + V1.
  • With modal verbs, place not after the modal: cannot, should not, will not.
  • Use negative words carefully and avoid unintended double negatives.
