Subject and Predicate: The Two Main Parts of a Sentence

Take a sentence like “The little boy is flying a kite.” We know who the sentence is about, and we know what he is doing. These two parts help us understand how a sentence is built.

In this lesson, we will learn how to find the subject and predicate in ordinary sentences, using examples from school, home and everyday life.

1. What is a subject?

The subject is the person, animal, thing or idea that a sentence is about. In many sentences, it tells us who is doing something or who or what is being described.

For example, in “Meena reads every evening,” the sentence is about Meena. So, Meena is the subject.

Sentence: The dog barked loudly.

Ask: Who barked loudly?

Answer: The dog.

So, “The dog” is the subject.

More examples

SentenceSubjectHow do we find it?
Rohan plays cricket.RohanWho plays cricket?

Remember that the subject is not always a person. It can be an animal, a place, a thing or even an idea.

2. What is a predicate?

The predicate is the part of a sentence that tells us something about the subject. It includes the verb and may also include other words that complete the message.

Look at this sentence: “The children played in the park.” The sentence is about the children. The words played in the park tell us what they did. These words form the predicate.

Sentence: My father drives carefully.

Subject: My father

Predicate: drives carefully

More examples

A predicate does not always describe an action. In “The sky is blue,” the predicate is blue tells us about the sky.

Let us divide the sentence “The little girl is watering the plants.”

Notice that the predicate is more than just the action word. Here, the verb phrase is “is watering”, but the full predicate is “is watering the plants”.

4. Simple subject and complete subject

Sometimes the subject is only one word. At other times, it includes describing words. It helps to know the difference between the simple subject and the complete subject.

The simple subject is the main word in the subject. The complete subject includes that main word and all the words that go with it.

SentenceComplete subjectSimple subject
The small brown puppy slept.The small brown puppypuppy
My best friend from school lives nearby.My best friend from schoolfriend
Several tall trees grow beside the road.Several tall treestrees
The new science teacher arrived today.The new science teacherteacher

In the first sentence, puppy is the main naming word. The words the small brown give us more information about the puppy.

5. Simple predicate and complete predicate

The simple predicate is the main verb or verb phrase. The complete predicate includes the verb and all the words that tell us more about the subject.

SentenceComplete predicateSimple predicate
The small brown puppy slept peacefully.slept peacefullyslept
My best friend from school lives nearby.lives nearbylives
The players have finished the match.have finished the matchhave finished
The new science teacher arrived today.arrived todayarrived

For school-level exercises, follow the terminology used by your textbook. Some books use “simple predicate” for the main verb, while others explain the verb phrase as a unit.

6. How to find the subject and predicate

Use these steps whenever you are unsure.

  1. Read the whole sentence. Make sure you understand its meaning.
  2. Find the verb. Look for the action or the word that links the subject to a description.
  3. Ask who or what? Ask who or what performs the action or is being described.
  4. Mark the subject. Include the words that belong with the main subject word.
  5. The remaining part is usually the predicate. Check that it tells us something about the subject.

Worked example

Sentence: The students in our class completed the project yesterday.

Verb: completed

Who completed the project? The students in our class.

Subject: The students in our class

Predicate: completed the project yesterday

Do not simply divide a sentence in the middle. Find its meaning first, then identify the subject and predicate.

7. Sentences with a hidden or understood subject

In a command or request, the subject you is often understood rather than written.

SentenceUnderstood subjectPredicate
Please sit down.YouPlease sit down
Open your notebook.YouOpen your notebook
Do not run in the corridor.YouDo not run in the corridor

These are commands. The speaker is talking to someone, so the subject you is understood.

8. Common mistakes to avoid

Common mistakeCorrect understanding
Calling only the verb the complete predicate.The complete predicate includes the verb and the other words that tell us about the subject.
Thinking the subject is always the first word.The subject often comes first, but questions and other sentence structures may place it elsewhere.
Thinking the subject is always a person.A subject can be an animal, thing, place or idea: “Rain fell all night.”
Forgetting helping verbs.In “The children have been studying,” the verb phrase includes “have been studying.”
Splitting a sentence without checking its meaning.Read the whole sentence and identify what it says before marking the two parts.
