Exclamatory Sentences: Rules and Examples

Exclamatory sentences express strong feelings such as joy, surprise, admiration, sadness or anger. Learn the common sentence patterns, when to use an exclamation mark and how exclamatory sentences differ from ordinary statements and questions.

1. What is an exclamatory sentence?

An exclamatory sentence expresses a strong emotion or reaction. It commonly ends with an exclamation mark (!).

  • What a beautiful rainbow! (admiration)
  • How exciting this match is! (excitement)
  • Oh no! I forgot my keys! (dismay)
  • That was an amazing performance! (enthusiasm)

Use exclamatory sentences when you want to show genuine feeling. Not every sentence needs an exclamation mark.

2. Exclamations with “what”

Use what in exclamations that focus on a noun or noun phrase.

PatternExample
What + a/an + adjective + singular countable noun!What a lovely day!
What + adjective + plural noun!What beautiful flowers!
What + adjective + uncountable noun!What wonderful news!

Remember to use a or an before a singular countable noun: What an interesting story! Do not use an article before a plural or uncountable noun in these patterns.

3. Exclamations with “how”

Use how to emphasise an adjective, an adverb or the degree of an action or quality.

PatternExample
How + adjective + subject + verb!How kind you are!
How + adverb + subject + verb!How quickly she runs!
How + adjective/adverb (informal exclamation)How wonderful!

Compare: What a clever student! and How clever the student is! Both express admiration but use different structures.

4. Other ways to express strong feelings

Not all exclamatory sentences begin with what or how. A statement or short expression can also be exclamatory when it conveys strong emotion.

  • That is fantastic!
  • I can't believe it!
  • Well done!
  • What a surprise!
  • Hurray! We won the match!
  • Alas! We have lost. (formal or literary expression of sadness)

Choose expressions that suit the context and your intended tone.

5. Exclamatory sentences and ordinary statements

A statement gives information, while an exclamatory sentence highlights a strong feeling. Sometimes the words are similar but the punctuation and tone differ.

StatementExclamatory sentence
The view is beautiful.How beautiful the view is!
It was a surprising result.What a surprising result!
She sings very well.How well she sings!
This is wonderful news.What wonderful news!

Do not confuse an exclamation with a question. How did she do it? is a question, but How brilliantly she did it! expresses admiration.

6. Punctuation and capital letters

  • Begin the sentence with a capital letter: What a lovely surprise!
  • Place the exclamation mark at the end of the exclamatory sentence.
  • Do not usually combine a question mark and an exclamation mark in formal school writing.
  • Use exclamation marks sparingly in formal essays and academic writing.

For example, write That is incredible! rather than That is incredible!!! in standard formal writing.

7. Changing statements into exclamatory sentences

One way to practise is to turn a statement into an exclamation while keeping its main meaning.

StatementPossible exclamation
The night is very quiet.How quiet the night is!
It is a beautiful painting.What a beautiful painting!
The child is very brave.How brave the child is!
They are wonderful friends.What wonderful friends they are!

There may be more than one correct way to express the same feeling. The key is to use a grammatical pattern and suitable punctuation.

8. Common mistakes

IncorrectCorrectReason
What beautiful a flower!What a beautiful flower!Use what + a/an + adjective + singular noun.
How a lovely day!What a lovely day!Use what with a noun phrase.
What an beautiful idea!What a beautiful idea!Choose a/an by the sound at the start of the next word.
How beautifully day!What a beautiful day!Use an adjective to describe a noun.
What a lovely day?What a lovely day!An exclamation normally ends with an exclamation mark.
How did she sing!How beautifully she sang!The original word order asks a question; use exclamatory word order to express admiration.

10. Quick revision

  • Exclamatory sentences express strong feelings and usually end with !
  • Use What + (a/an) + adjective + noun for noun-focused exclamations.
  • Use How + adjective/adverb to emphasise a quality or action.
  • Start the sentence with a capital letter and avoid excessive exclamation marks.
  • Check whether a sentence is asking a question or expressing a feeling; word order and punctuation help show the difference.
