Words and Sentences: The First Step in English Grammar

Every time you ask a question, tell a story or explain something to a friend, you use words to share a thought. Grammar helps those words work together clearly.

Let’s begin with the kind of English we use every day—at home, in the classroom and with our friends.

1. What is a word?

A word is a unit of language that carries meaning or performs a job in a sentence. We use words to name people and things, describe them, show actions, ask questions and express feelings.

Look around your classroom. You may see a teacher, a book, a desk and a window. These words name things. In the sentence “The teacher smiles,” the word smiles tells us what the teacher does.

WordWhat it tells usExample
teacherA personThe teacher is kind.
bookA thingThis book is new.
runAn actionChildren run in the park.
happyA quality or feelingI feel happy.
slowlyHow an action happensShe walks slowly.

You will learn the grammar names for these groups later. For now, focus on what each word does.

2. What is a sentence?

A sentence is a group of words arranged to express a complete thought. In writing, a sentence usually begins with a capital letter and ends with suitable punctuation.

Complete thought: “The bus has arrived.”

Not a complete thought by itself: “Because the bus…”

The second group makes us wait for the rest of the message. We expect to hear what happened because the bus.

Compare these examples:

WordsComplete sentence?Why?
My sister is reading.YesIt gives a complete message.
In the school bagNo, by itselfIt tells us a place, but not a complete thought.
Where is my pencil?YesIt asks a complete question.
Please open your book.YesIt gives a complete instruction.

A sentence does not always need to be long. “Stop!” is a complete command in the right situation.

3. A simple sentence has a structure

Many simple English sentences tell us who or what we are talking about and what we say about that person or thing. The first part is often called the subject; the part that says something about it is the predicate. We will study both in the next lesson.

Ask, “Who reads?” The answer is “Riya”. What does Riya do? She “reads a book”. That is the basic idea.

4. Common sentence patterns

Here are some useful patterns. They are not every possible sentence pattern, but they are a good place to begin.

PatternExampleWhat to notice
Person or thing + actionBirds fly.“Birds” is what we talk about; “fly” tells the action.
Person + action + thingRiya reads a book.We know the person, action and thing.
Person + linking verb + descriptionMy brother is cheerful.“Is” connects the subject with a description.
There + be verb + nounThere are two chairs.This tells us that something is present.

Do not worry about memorising the labels yet. Read each example aloud and notice how the words fit together.

5. Capital letters and punctuation

Capital letters and punctuation help readers understand where a sentence begins, where it ends and what the writer intends.

UseExample
Capital letter at the beginningWe play cricket.
Full stop for a statementThe class starts at nine.
Question mark for a questionAre you ready?
Exclamation mark to show strong feeling or emphasisWhat a lovely day!
Capitalise names of people and placesRiya lives in Jaipur.

6. Common mistakes to fix

IncorrectCorrectReason
riya reads a bookRiya reads a book.Start with a capital letter and end with punctuation.
My friend happy.My friend is happy.In this sentence, “is” connects the subject with its description.
Because I was late.I missed the bus because I was late.The first version often leaves the reader waiting for the main message.

Context matters: a fragment can be used deliberately in conversation or creative writing, but learners should first practise complete standard sentences.

7. Try these questions

Read each question, write your answer, and then open the answer to check it.

Question 1

Which of the following is a complete sentence?

  1. Under the big tree
  2. The children are playing.
  3. Because it was raining
Show answer

Option 2: “The children are playing.” It gives a complete message.

Question 2

Correct this sentence: “my father works in jaipur”

Show answer

My father works in Jaipur.

Question 3

In “Aman kicks the ball,” who performs the action?

Show answer

Aman.

Question 4

Is “Please sit down.” a sentence? Why?

Show answer

Yes. It is a command that communicates a complete instruction.

Question 5

Add the correct punctuation: “Where are you going”

Show answer

Where are you going?

8. A little practice for your notebook

  1. Write five words you see around you and use each in a sentence.
  2. Write three statements, two questions and one command.
  3. Underline the person or thing each sentence is about in: “The little puppy sleeps near the door.”
  4. Correct the capital letters and punctuation in: “we visited delhi on monday”

Grammar becomes easier when you notice it in your own words. Try making examples about your family, classroom, friends or daily routine.
