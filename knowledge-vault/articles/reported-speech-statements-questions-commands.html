Reported Speech: Statements, Questions and Commands

Reported speech lets us tell someone what another person said without repeating the exact words. Learn how to report statements, questions, requests and commands by changing pronouns, verb forms and time expressions where necessary.

1. What Are Direct and Reported Speech?

Direct speech repeats a speaker's exact words, usually inside quotation marks. Reported speech gives the meaning of those words without quoting them exactly.

  • Direct: Riya said, “I am tired.”
  • Reported: Riya said that she was tired.

Notice that the pronoun changes from I to she, and the verb changes from am to was because the reporting verb is in the past.

2. Reporting Statements

To report a statement, we commonly use said (that) or told + object (that). The word that is often optional.

  • Direct: Aman said, “I like football.”
  • Reported: Aman said (that) he liked football.
  • Direct: Meera said to me, “I am busy.”
  • Reported: Meera told me (that) she was busy.

Remember the pattern: said + (that) + statement; told + person + (that) + statement. Do not normally say “told that” without mentioning the person who was told.

3. Common Tense Changes

When the reporting verb is in the past, the tense in the reported clause often moves one step back. This is called backshift. The change is common in school grammar exercises, but it is not always necessary when the information remains true or the context does not require it.

Direct speechReported speech
Simple present: “I work.”Simple past: He said that he worked.
Present continuous: “I am working.”Past continuous: He said that he was working.
Present perfect: “I have finished.”Past perfect: He said that he had finished.
Simple past: “I saw her.”Past perfect: He said that he had seen her.
Will: “I will call.”Would: He said that he would call.
Can: “I can swim.”Could: He said that he could swim.
May: “I may come.”Might: He said that he might come.

4. Verb Forms That Often Stay the Same

Some forms do not normally change, and context matters.

  • Past perfect: “I had left.” → She said that she had left.
  • Would, could, should, might: “I could help.” → He said that he could help.
  • General truths: “Water boils at 100°C at standard atmospheric pressure.” → The teacher explained that water boils at 100°C at standard atmospheric pressure.
  • Still true information: “I live in Jaipur.” → She said that she lives in Jaipur. (If she still lives there.)

For examination transformations, follow the tense-change rules expected by your syllabus, while remembering that natural English can preserve a tense when the meaning supports it.

5. Changing Pronouns and Possessives

Pronouns change according to who spoke and who is reporting the words. They do not change mechanically in every sentence; check the context.

  • Direct: Ravi said, “I have lost my pen.”
  • Reported: Ravi said that he had lost his pen.
  • Direct: Neha said to me, “You are helpful.”
  • Reported: Neha told me that I was helpful.
  • Direct: The children said, “We are ready.”
  • Reported: The children said that they were ready.

Ask: Who is speaking? Who is being addressed? Who is reporting the statement? These questions help you choose the correct pronouns.

7. Reporting Yes/No Questions

Use asked and if or whether to report a yes/no question. Change the question word order to statement word order, and do not keep the question mark at the end of the reported clause.

  • Direct: He asked me, “Are you ready?”
  • Reported: He asked me if I was ready.
  • Direct: She asked, “Do you like tea?”
  • Reported: She asked whether I liked tea.
  • Direct: Ravi asked her, “Can you swim?”
  • Reported: Ravi asked her if she could swim.

Do not use do or does in the reported clause unless it is needed for the meaning of the statement. “He asked where did I live” is incorrect; use “He asked where I lived.”

8. Reporting WH-Questions

When a direct question begins with who, what, where, when, why or how, keep the question word but use statement word order in reported speech.

  • Direct: She asked me, “Where do you live?”
  • Reported: She asked me where I lived.
  • Direct: He asked, “Why are you crying?”
  • Reported: He asked why I was crying.
  • Direct: The teacher asked, “When did you finish?”
  • Reported: The teacher asked when I had finished.

The reported question is usually a statement in word order, so it does not end with a question mark when embedded in a reporting sentence.

9. Reporting Commands, Requests and Advice

Commands and requests are commonly reported with told, ordered, asked, requested, advised or another suitable reporting verb, followed by an object and to + base verb.

  • Direct: The teacher said to us, “Open your books.”
  • Reported: The teacher told us to open our books.
  • Direct: She said to me, “Please help me.”
  • Reported: She asked me to help her.
  • Direct: The doctor said to him, “Take some rest.”
  • Reported: The doctor advised him to take some rest.

Choose a reporting verb that matches the intention and tone rather than using told for every sentence.

10. Reporting Negative Commands and Requests

For a negative command or request, use not to + base verb.

  • Direct: The guard said, “Don't enter.”
  • Reported: The guard warned us not to enter.
  • Direct: Mother said to me, “Don't waste time.”
  • Reported: Mother told me not to waste time.
  • Direct: He said to them, “Please don't shout.”
  • Reported: He asked them not to shout.

11. Reporting Suggestions and Exclamations

Suggestions can be reported with suggested that or suggested + verb-ing, depending on the structure.

  • Direct: He said, “Let's take a break.”
  • Reported: He suggested taking a break.
  • Reported alternative: He suggested that we should take a break.

Exclamations can be reported by describing the feeling or reaction.

  • Direct: She said, “What a beautiful view!”
  • Reported: She exclaimed that the view was very beautiful.
  • Direct: He said, “Hurrah! We have won!”
  • Reported: He exclaimed with joy that they had won.

12. “Say” and “Tell”: Common Differences

PatternExample
say + wordsHe said that he was tired.
say to + personHe said to me that he was tired.
tell + personHe told me that he was tired.
ask + person + if/whetherShe asked me if I was ready.
ask + person + to-infinitiveShe asked me to wait.

Common error: “He told that he was tired.” In the usual reported-speech pattern, write “He said that he was tired” or “He told me that he was tired.”

13. A Simple Method for Converting Direct Speech

  1. Identify whether the sentence is a statement, a question, a command or a request.
  2. Choose a suitable reporting verb: said, told, asked, requested, advised and so on.
  3. Change pronouns according to the speaker, listener and reporting situation.
  4. Apply the appropriate tense change if the reporting verb is in the past and backshift is needed.
  5. Change time and place expressions only when the context requires it.
  6. For reported questions, use statement word order.
  7. For commands, use to + verb; for negative commands, use not to + verb.
  8. Check punctuation and remove quotation marks from the reported clause.

15. Practice Exercise A: Report the Statements

  1. Rita said, “I am happy.”
  2. Aman said, “I have finished my homework.”
  3. They said, “We will visit Jaipur tomorrow.”
  4. He said, “I can solve this question.”
  5. Meena said, “I saw a rainbow yesterday.”
Show answers
  1. Rita said that she was happy.
  2. Aman said that he had finished his homework.
  3. They said that they would visit Jaipur the following day.
  4. He said that he could solve that question.
  5. Meena said that she had seen a rainbow the previous day.

16. Practice Exercise B: Report the Questions

  1. She asked me, “Do you like music?”
  2. He asked, “Where are you going?”
  3. The teacher asked Ravi, “Why were you absent?”
  4. Mother asked me, “Have you eaten?”
Show answers
  1. She asked me if I liked music.
  2. He asked where I was going.
  3. The teacher asked Ravi why he had been absent.
  4. Mother asked me whether I had eaten.

17. Practice Exercise C: Report the Commands and Requests

  1. The coach said to the players, “Run faster.”
  2. She said to me, “Please wait here.”
  3. The librarian said to us, “Don't make a noise.”
  4. The doctor said to him, “Drink more water.”
Show answers
  1. The coach told the players to run faster.
  2. She asked me to wait there.
  3. The librarian told us not to make a noise.
  4. The doctor advised him to drink more water.

18. Quick Revision

  • Direct speech quotes exact words; reported speech conveys the meaning.
  • Use said for statements and told + person when naming the listener.
  • Past reporting verbs often lead to tense backshift, but context matters.
  • Pronouns and time expressions change according to who reports the words and when.
  • Report yes/no questions with if or whether.
  • Keep statement word order in reported questions.
  • Report commands with to + verb and negative commands with not to + verb.

Practise by changing one direct sentence at a time. First identify its purpose, then adjust the reporting verb, word order and other details carefully.
