Present Continuous Tense: Rules and Examples

The present continuous tense helps us describe actions that are happening now or around the present time. Learn how to use am, is and are with the -ing form of a verb.

1. What is the present continuous tense?

The present continuous tense, also called the present progressive tense, describes an action that is in progress now or around the time of speaking.

  • I am reading a book right now.
  • She is learning French this month.
  • They are playing in the park.

Its basic structure is:

Subject + am/is/are + verb-ing

2. Affirmative sentence structure

SubjectHelping verbExample
IamI am writing.
He / She / It / singular nounisRavi is writing.
You / We / They / plural nounareThey are writing.

Choose the correct helping verb according to the subject, then add the -ing form of the main verb.

3. Negative sentences

To make a negative sentence, add not after am, is or are.

PositiveNegative
I am working.I am not working.
She is sleeping.She is not (isn't) sleeping.
We are waiting.We are not (aren't) waiting.

Common contractions are isn't for is not and aren't for are not. In standard English, we normally say I'm not, rather than contracting am not as “amn’t”.

4. Questions

For a yes/no question, place am, is or are before the subject.

StatementQuestion
You are listening.Are you listening?
He is studying.Is he studying?
I am speaking too quickly.Am I speaking too quickly?

For questions with question words, put the question word first:

  • What are you doing?
  • Where is she going?
  • Why are they laughing?

Short answers: Yes, I am. / No, I am not. / Yes, she is. / No, they aren't.

5. Spelling rules for the -ing form

RuleBase verb-ing form
Usually add -ingread, play, workreading, playing, working
Drop final silent -emake, write, comemaking, writing, coming
Keep -eesee, agreeseeing, agreeing
Double final consonant in many short consonant-vowel-consonant verbsrun, sit, swimrunning, sitting, swimming
Change -ie to -ylie, dielying, dying
Keep -ystudy, enjoystudying, enjoying

Spelling can depend on stress and word patterns. For example, open → opening, but begin → beginning.

6. When do we use the present continuous?

Actions happening now

  • The children are drawing pictures.
  • I am talking to my teacher.

Temporary situations around the present

  • She is staying with her aunt this week.
  • We are studying a new chapter this month.

Changing or developing situations

  • The days are getting longer.
  • Technology is changing quickly.

Arrangements in the near future

  • I am meeting my friend tomorrow.
  • They are travelling to Delhi on Friday.

Repeated behaviour with “always”

We can use the present continuous with always to show irritation or strong feeling.

  • He is always losing his keys!

7. Present simple or present continuous?

Simple presentPresent continuous
I read every evening. (habit)I am reading now. (action in progress)
She works at a school. (regular situation)She is working from home this week. (temporary situation)
The train leaves at six. (timetable)We are leaving at six. (arrangement)

Some verbs describe states rather than actions and are not usually used in continuous forms. These include know, believe, understand, own and belong.

  • Correct: I understand the question.
  • Usually incorrect: I am understanding the question.

Some verbs can be used in both forms with different meanings, so always consider the context.

8. Common mistakes

IncorrectCorrectReason

He is makeing tea.He is making tea.Drop the silent e before -ing.

10. Quick revision

  • Structure: subject + am/is/are + verb-ing.
  • Use am with I, is with he/she/it and are with you/we/they.
  • For negatives, add not after am/is/are.
  • For questions, move am/is/are before the subject.
  • Use the tense for actions in progress, temporary situations, changes and future arrangements.
  • Do not usually use continuous forms with stative verbs such as know and believe.
