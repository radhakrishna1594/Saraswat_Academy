Simple Present Tense: Rules and Examples

We use the simple present tense to talk about habits, routines, facts and situations that are generally true. Learn how to make positive sentences, negative sentences and questions correctly.

1. What is the simple present tense?

The simple present tense describes habits, repeated actions, facts, general truths and some long-lasting situations.

  • I wake up at six every morning. (routine)
  • Water boils at 100°C at standard atmospheric pressure. (scientific fact)
  • She lives in Jaipur. (current situation)
  • The school opens at 8 a.m. (schedule)

2. Sentence structure

Affirmative sentences

SubjectStructureExample
I / You / We / TheySubject + V1They play cricket.
He / She / It / singular nounSubject + V1 with -s/-esRiya plays cricket.

With I, you, we and they, use the base verb. With he, she, it or a singular noun, usually add -s or -es.

Negative sentences

SubjectStructureExample
I / You / We / TheySubject + do not + V1We do not eat late.
He / She / It / singular nounSubject + does not + V1He does not eat late.

Use do not (don't) with I/you/we/they and does not (doesn't) with he/she/it and singular nouns. After doesn't, the main verb returns to V1: She doesn't like tea, not She doesn't likes tea.

Questions

Question patternExample
Do + I/you/we/they + V1…?Do you read daily?
Does + he/she/it + V1…?Does she read daily?
Question word + do/does + subject + V1…?Where does he work?

Short answers: Yes, I do. / No, I don't. / Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.

4. When do we use the simple present?

Habits and routines

  • I brush my teeth twice a day.
  • My father walks every evening.

Facts and general truths

  • The Earth moves around the Sun.
  • Plants need water and sunlight to grow.

Long-lasting situations

  • They live near the school.
  • He works at a bank.

Timetables and fixed schedules

  • The train leaves at 6:30 tomorrow morning.
  • The examination begins on Monday.

Instructions and directions

  • First, you mix the flour and water.
  • Turn left at the traffic lights.

5. Common time expressions

These expressions often appear with the simple present tense.

ExpressionExample
alwaysShe always arrives on time.
usuallyWe usually eat at home.
oftenHe often visits his grandparents.
sometimesI sometimes walk to school.
rarely / seldomThey rarely watch television.
every day / every weekShe practises every day.

Adverbs of frequency usually come before the main verb: He often reads. With the verb be, they usually come after it: She is always kind.

6. The verb “be” in the simple present

The verb be has special forms: am, is, are. We do not use do/does to make ordinary negatives or questions with these forms.

PositiveNegativeQuestion
I am ready.I am not ready.Am I ready?
He is happy.He is not happy.Is he happy?
They are at home.They are not at home.Are they at home?

Say Is she tired?, not Does she be tired?

8. Practice exercises

Choose the correct form or correct the sentence. Try each question before opening the answer.

  1. My mother (cook / cooks) dinner every evening.
    Show answer

    cooks — “My mother” is singular.

  2. They (doesn't / don't) play tennis on Sundays.
    Show answer

    don't — Use do not with “they”.

  3. ___ your brother speak English? (Do / Does)
    Show answer

    Does your brother speak English?

  4. Correct the sentence: She go to the library every week.
    Show answer

    She goes to the library every week.

  5. Correct the sentence: Does he likes mangoes?
    Show answer

    Does he like mangoes?

  6. Water (boil / boils) at 100°C at standard atmospheric pressure.
    Show answer

    boils — “Water” is treated as a singular uncountable noun.

  7. Make this sentence negative: Rohan watches television.
    Show answer

    Rohan does not (doesn't) watch television.

  8. Make a question: You study English.
    Show answer

    Do you study English?

  9. Choose the correct form: My friends (live / lives) in Jaipur.
    Show answer

    live — “My friends” is plural.

  10. Complete the sentence: She ___ a new school bag. (have / has)
    Show answer

    has — The simple present form of “have” with she is “has”.

9. Quick revision

  • Use the simple present for routines, habits, facts and regular situations.
  • Use V1 with I, you, we and they.
  • Add -s or -es to the verb with he, she, it and singular nouns in affirmative statements.
  • Use don't/doesn't + V1 for negatives.
  • Use Do/Does + subject + V1 for questions.
  • With the verb be, use am/is/are to make statements, negatives and questions.
