Direct and Indirect Speech: Rules and Examples

We often repeat what someone has said. Learn how to report another person's words accurately using direct speech and indirect speech, with clear rules and examples.

1. What Is Direct Speech?

Direct speech gives a person's exact words. The words spoken are placed inside quotation marks.

  • Riya said, “I am tired.”
  • The teacher said, “The Earth moves around the Sun.”
  • Arun asked me, “Have you finished your homework?”

The reporting clause tells us who spoke, for example, Riya said or the teacher asked.

2. What Is Indirect Speech?

Indirect speech (also called reported speech) reports the meaning of someone's words without repeating the exact wording. Quotation marks are not used.

  • Direct: Riya said, “I am tired.”
  • Indirect: Riya said that she was tired.
  • Direct: Arun asked me, “Have you finished your homework?”
  • Indirect: Arun asked me whether I had finished my homework.

When changing direct speech into indirect speech, we may need to adjust pronouns, verb tense, time expressions and sentence structure.

3. Main Rules for Changing Direct Speech into Indirect Speech

Rule 1: Remove Quotation Marks

Indirect speech does not use quotation marks around the reported words.

Direct: She said, “I am ready.”
Indirect: She said that she was ready.

Rule 2: Change Pronouns Where Necessary

Pronouns change according to the speaker, the listener and the context. Do not change them mechanically; identify who each pronoun refers to.

Direct: Mohan said, “I have lost my pen.”
Indirect: Mohan said that he had lost his pen.

Rule 3: Change the Tense When Required

If the reporting verb is in the past, the tense of the reported clause often moves one step back when reporting a statement about a past or current situation.

4. Reporting Statements

For statements, we commonly use said that or told + object + that. The word that can often be omitted in everyday English.

  • Direct: Neha said, “I like music.”
    Indirect: Neha said that she liked music.
  • Direct: He said to me, “You are helpful.”
    Indirect: He told me that I was helpful.
  • Direct: The teacher said, “Water boils at 100°C at standard atmospheric pressure.”
    Indirect: The teacher said that water boils at 100°C at standard atmospheric pressure.

Important: Use told with an object: He told me that… Do not write He told that… without an object in this construction.

5. Reporting Yes/No Questions

For a question that can be answered with yes or no, use if or whether. Use statement word order in the reported clause and do not keep the question mark in the middle of the sentence.

  • Direct: Sara asked me, “Are you busy?”
    Indirect: Sara asked me if I was busy.
  • Direct: He asked, “Did you see the match?”
    Indirect: He asked whether I had seen the match.
  • Direct: Mother asked me, “Have you eaten?”
    Indirect: Mother asked me if I had eaten.

Do not use question word order after if or whether. Write if I was busy, not if was I busy.

6. Reporting Wh-Questions

When a question begins with what, where, when, why, who, whom or how, keep the question word. Change the word order to a statement structure.

  • Direct: She asked me, “Where do you live?”
    Indirect: She asked me where I lived.
  • Direct: The teacher asked, “Why are you late?”
    Indirect: The teacher asked why I was late.
  • Direct: He asked her, “How did you solve it?”
    Indirect: He asked her how she had solved it.

In indirect questions, do not use do or does as question-support verbs in the reported clause.

7. Reporting Commands, Requests and Advice

Commands and requests are commonly reported with told, ordered, asked, requested, advised or another suitable reporting verb, followed by an object and to + verb. Negative commands often use not to + verb.

  • Direct: The teacher said to us, “Open your books.”
    Indirect: The teacher told us to open our books.
  • Direct: She said to me, “Please help me.”
    Indirect: She asked me to help her.
  • Direct: Father said to me, “Do not waste time.”
    Indirect: Father advised me not to waste time.
  • Direct: The officer said to the driver, “Stop the car.”
    Indirect: The officer ordered the driver to stop the car.

Choose the reporting verb that matches the speaker's intention. A polite request is not necessarily an order.

8. Reporting Exclamations and Wishes

Exclamations may be reported by describing the feeling or using verbs such as exclaimed, shouted, wished or prayed, depending on the meaning.

  • Direct: She said, “What a beautiful painting!”
    Indirect: She exclaimed that it was a very beautiful painting.
  • Direct: He said, “Hurrah! We have won the match.”
    Indirect: He exclaimed with joy that they had won the match.
  • Direct: Mother said, “May you succeed!”
    Indirect: Mother wished that I might succeed.

There may be more than one natural way to report an exclamation. Preserve the original emotion and meaning.

9. When Does the Tense Not Change?

  • Universal truths: Direct: The teacher said, “The Earth revolves around the Sun.” Indirect: The teacher said that the Earth revolves around the Sun.
  • Continuing facts or situations: A tense may remain the same when the statement is still true and the context supports it.
  • Present reporting verb: Direct: He says, “I am hungry.” Indirect: He says that he is hungry.

In school exercises, follow the tense-shift pattern expected by the question when the reporting verb is in the past, unless the sentence clearly gives a reason to keep the tense.

10. Common Mistakes

  • Incorrect: He told that he was ill. Correct: He told me that he was ill. / He said that he was ill.
  • Incorrect: She asked me where did I live. Correct: She asked me where I lived.
  • Incorrect: He asked that whether I was ready. Correct: He asked whether I was ready.
  • Incorrect: The teacher told us do the work. Correct: The teacher told us to do the work.
  • Incorrect: She requested me help her. Correct: She requested me to help her. More naturally: She asked me to help her.

11. Practice Exercise A: Change into Indirect Speech

  1. Ravi said, “I am learning English.”
  2. Meena said to me, “I will call you tomorrow.”
  3. The teacher said, “The Earth revolves around the Sun.”
  4. He asked me, “Do you know the answer?”
  5. She asked him, “Why are you laughing?”
  6. Mother said to me, “Please switch off the fan.”
  7. The coach said to the players, “Do not give up.”
  8. They exclaimed, “What a wonderful performance!”
Show sample answers
  1. Ravi said that he was learning English.
  2. Meena told me that she would call me the next day.
  3. The teacher said that the Earth revolves around the Sun.
  4. He asked me whether I knew the answer.
  5. She asked him why he was laughing.
  6. Mother asked me to switch off the fan.
  7. The coach advised the players not to give up.
  8. They exclaimed that it was a wonderful performance.

Pronouns and time expressions may vary depending on who is reporting and when the report is made.

12. Practice Exercise B: Change into Direct Speech

  1. Anita said that she was feeling better.
  2. He asked me if I liked cricket.
  3. The teacher told the students to sit down.
  4. Father advised me not to be careless.
Show sample answers
  1. Anita said, “I am feeling better.”
  2. He asked me, “Do you like cricket?”
  3. The teacher said to the students, “Sit down.”
  4. Father said to me, “Do not be careless.”

Direct speech can have more than one correct version if the original words are not fully specified.

13. Quick Revision

  • Direct speech repeats exact words inside quotation marks.
  • Indirect speech reports meaning without quotation marks.
  • Pronouns and time or place expressions change according to context.
  • Past reporting verbs often cause a tense backshift, but universal truths and continuing facts may keep their tense.
  • Use if/whether for yes/no questions and keep statement word order in reported questions.
  • Use object + to-infinitive for many commands and requests.

The best way to master reported speech is to identify the sentence type first, then apply the rule that fits it.
