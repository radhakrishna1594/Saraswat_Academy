Pronouns in English Grammar: Types and Examples

Imagine saying, “Riya is my friend. Riya is kind. I study with Riya.” The sentence sounds repetitive. We can make it smoother: “Riya is my friend. She is kind. I study with her.” Words such as she and her are pronouns. They help us speak and write clearly without repeating the same noun again and again.

1. What is a pronoun?

A pronoun is a word used in place of a noun or noun phrase. The noun that a pronoun refers to is called its antecedent.

Without a pronoun: Aman has a bicycle. Aman rides Aman’s bicycle every day.

Better: Aman has a bicycle. He rides his bicycle every day.

Here, he and his refer to Aman.

Use a pronoun only when the reader or listener can understand whom or what it refers to.

2. Personal pronouns

Personal pronouns refer to people, animals or things. Their form changes according to the person, number and job they do in a sentence.

PersonSubject formObject formExample
First person singularImeI called Neha. Neha called me.
Second personyouyouYou helped you is not a natural meaning; say, “I helped you.”
Third person singularhe, she, ithim, her, itShe thanked him.
First person pluralweusWe invited them to join us.
Third person pluraltheythemThey saw us.

Subject pronouns usually do the action: I read. They laughed.

Object pronouns receive the action or follow a preposition: She called me. Come with us.

3. Possessive pronouns and possessive determiners

These words show ownership. Some stand alone, while others come before a noun.

Before a noun (determiner)Stands alone (pronoun)Example
mymineThis is my bag. The bag is mine.
youryoursIs this your pen? Is it yours?
hishisThat is his seat. The seat is his.
herhersThis is her book. The book is hers.
ouroursIt is our classroom. The classroom is ours.
theirtheirsThese are their shoes. The shoes are theirs.

Do not write an apostrophe in yours, hers, ours or theirs. “Your’s” and “her’s” are incorrect.

4. Reflexive pronouns

Reflexive pronouns end in -self or -selves. They are used when the subject and the person or thing affected are the same.

PronounExample
myselfI taught myself to cook.
yourselfPlease take care of yourself.
himself / herself / itselfThe cat cleaned itself.
ourselvesWe prepared ourselves for the test.
yourselvesYou should be proud of yourselves.
themselvesThe children organised themselves.

Common mistake: “My friend and myself went to school” is not standard in this meaning. Write “My friend and I went to school.”

5. Demonstrative pronouns

This, that, these and those point to people or things.

WordUsual useExample
thisone thing nearbyThis is my notebook.
thatone thing farther awayThat is our school bus.
thesemore than one nearbyThese are fresh apples.
thosemore than one farther awayThose are my friends.

When these words come directly before a noun—“this notebook”, “those apples”—many grammar books call them demonstrative determiners.

6. Interrogative pronouns

Interrogative pronouns are used to ask questions.

PronounWhat it asks aboutExample
whoa person as subjectWho is at the door?
whoma person as object, especially in formal EnglishWhom did you invite?
whoseownershipWhose is this?
whata thing or informationWhat happened?
whicha choice from a setWhich is yours?

7. Relative pronouns

Relative pronouns connect extra information to a noun or pronoun. Common examples are who, whom, whose, which and that.

  • The girl who won the race is my cousin.
  • This is the book that I borrowed.
  • I met a writer whose stories I enjoy.
  • The phone, which was on the table, belongs to Dad.

Use who mainly for people and which for things. That can refer to people or things in many defining clauses.

8. Indefinite and reciprocal pronouns

Indefinite pronouns refer to people or things without naming them exactly: someone, anyone, everyone, nobody, something, many, few and several.

  • Someone left a notebook here.
  • Everyone is ready.
  • Several were absent today.

Words such as each other and one another are reciprocal pronouns. They show that people do the same action to one another.

Example: The two friends helped each other.

9. Keep the pronoun clear

A pronoun should agree with the noun it refers to and should not leave the reader guessing.

Unclear: When Mohan spoke to Ravi, he was upset.

Who was upset—Mohan or Ravi? The sentence does not tell us clearly.

Clearer: Mohan was upset when he spoke to Ravi.

In modern English, singular they is also commonly used when a person's gender is unknown or when that is the person's pronoun: “Someone left their umbrella.”

10. Practice questions

Try these questions before opening the answers.

  1. Find the pronoun: “Meera said that she was ready.”
  2. Replace the repeated noun: “The boys are here. The boys are waiting.”
  3. Choose the correct word: “This pen is (my / mine).”
  4. Choose the correct word: “Rohan and (I / me) went to the library.”
  5. Fill in the blank with a reflexive pronoun: “She made the cake by ______.”
  6. Choose a relative pronoun: “The man ______ helped us was kind.”
  7. Identify the demonstrative pronoun: “Those are beautiful flowers.”
  8. Rewrite to make the reference clear: “When Asha met Pooja, she smiled.”
Show answers and explanations
  1. she — it refers to Meera.
  2. “The boys are here. They are waiting.”
  3. mine — it stands alone without a noun after it. “This is my pen” would also be correct.
  4. I — it is part of the subject: “Rohan and I went…”
  5. herself.
  6. who or that.
  7. Those.
  8. Possible answer: “Asha smiled when she met Pooja.” If Pooja smiled, write “Pooja smiled when Asha met her.” The intended meaning must be clear.
