Simple Past Tense: Rules and Examples

We use the simple past tense to talk about actions and situations that happened or were true at a finished time in the past. Learn the rules for regular and irregular verbs, negatives and questions.

1. What is the simple past tense?

The simple past tense describes an action or situation that happened and finished in the past.

  • We visited our grandparents yesterday.
  • She watched a film last night.
  • He left school at three o'clock.
  • They were happy after the match.

Common time expressions include yesterday, last week, two days ago, in 2022 and when I was young.

2. Affirmative sentences

For most verbs, use V2 (the simple past form) for all subjects.

SubjectStructureExample
I / You / He / She / ItSubject + V2She played chess.
We / They / plural nounsSubject + V2They played chess.

Unlike the simple present, the main verb does not change according to the subject: I worked, he worked, and they worked.

3. Regular verbs and spelling rules

Regular verbs generally form the simple past by adding -ed or -d.

RuleV1V2
Usually add -edwork, cleanworked, cleaned
Verb ends in silent -e: add -dlive, dancelived, danced
Consonant + y: change y to -iedstudy, carrystudied, carried
Vowel + y: add -edplay, enjoyplayed, enjoyed
Some short verbs double the final consonantstop, planstopped, planned

Examples: We studied for the test. He stopped the car. They enjoyed the trip.

5. Negative sentences

Use did not (didn't) + V1 to make most negative sentences. The word did already shows past time, so the main verb returns to its base form.

PositiveNegative
She played tennis.She did not play tennis.
He went to school.He didn't go to school.
They ate breakfast.They didn't eat breakfast.

The verb be is different: use was not or were not, not didn't be.

  • I wasn't late.
  • They weren't at home.

6. Questions

For most verbs, form a yes/no question with Did + subject + V1.

StatementQuestion
You watched the match.Did you watch the match?
She wrote a letter.Did she write a letter?
They went home.Did they go home?

Question words can come before did:

  • Where did you stay?
  • What did he buy?
  • When did they arrive?

With be, put was/were before the subject: Was she tired? Were they ready?

Short answers: Yes, I did. / No, I didn't. / Yes, she was. / No, they weren't.

7. When do we use the simple past?

Completed actions

  • I finished the assignment yesterday.
  • They cleaned the classroom after school.

A sequence of past events

  • She woke up, ate breakfast and left for school.

Past habits or repeated actions

  • When I was a child, I played outside every evening.

Past states

  • My grandfather lived in a village.
  • The weather was cold last night.

8. Simple past or present perfect?

Simple pastPresent perfect
I visited Jaipur last year. (Finished time.)I have visited Jaipur several times. (Experience up to now.)
She lost her keys yesterday.She has lost her keys. (The result matters now.)
We finished at six.We have finished. (The task is complete now.)

10. Practice exercises

Try each question before opening its answer.

  1. Complete: We ___ (visit) the museum yesterday.
    Show answer

  2. Write the past form of “go”.
    Show answer

    went.

  3. Choose: She (buy / bought) a new bag last week.
    Show answer

    bought — “Buy” is irregular.

  4. Make negative: He played football.
    Show answer

    He did not (didn't) play football.

  5. Make a question: They watched the film.
    Show answer

    Did they watch the film?

  6. Correct: Did you went there?
    Show answer

    Did you go there?

  7. Complete: I ___ (be) tired yesterday.
    Show answer

    I was tired yesterday.

  8. Complete: The children ___ (play) in the garden last evening.
    Show answer

    The children played in the garden last evening.

  9. Choose: We (saw / have seen) him yesterday.
    Show answer

    saw — use simple past with “yesterday”.

  10. Correct: She studyed for the exam.
    Show answer

    She studied for the exam.

11. Quick revision

  • Use V2 for affirmative sentences in the simple past.
  • Use did not/didn't + V1 for negatives.
  • Use Did + subject + V1 for questions.
  • Use was/were with the verb be.
  • Regular verbs usually take -ed/-d; irregular verbs have special forms.
  • Use the simple past with finished past times such as yesterday, last week and in 2020.
