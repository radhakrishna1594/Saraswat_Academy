Articles and Determiners in English Grammar

Words such as a, the, this, some and many help us identify or describe a noun. Learn how to use them correctly with simple rules and examples.

1. What are determiners?

A determiner is a word placed before a noun to show which person or thing we mean, how much or how many we mean, or who it belongs to.

  • This book is interesting.
  • I have some water.
  • Her bag is blue.
  • Three students are absent.

In these examples, this, some, her and three help us understand the nouns more clearly.

2. Articles: a, an and the

Articles are a small group of determiners. English has three articles: a, an and the.

A and an: talking about one non-specific thing

Use a or an with a singular countable noun when we are not identifying a particular person or thing.

ArticleWhen to use itExamples
aBefore a consonant sounda book, a teacher, a university
anBefore a vowel soundan apple, an hour, an umbrella

Remember: Choose a or an according to the sound at the beginning of the next word, not simply its first letter.

  • She is a useful member of the team. (“Useful” begins with a /y/ sound.)
  • He waited for an hour. (The “h” is silent.)
  • Riya bought an orange and a banana.

The: talking about a particular thing

Use the when the listener or reader can identify the person or thing being discussed.

  • Please close the door. (The particular door is understood.)
  • The sun rises in the east.
  • I read a story yesterday. The story was funny. (Now we know which story.)

When no article is needed

We often use no article when speaking generally about plural nouns or uncountable nouns.

  • Books can open new worlds. (Books in general.)
  • Milk is rich in calcium. (Milk in general.)
  • My sister studies English.

Compare: I like music (music in general) and I like the music in this film (specific music).

3. Common types of determiners

TypeCommon wordsExample
Articlesa, an, theI saw a bird.
Demonstrativesthis, that, these, thoseThose shoes are new.
Possessivesmy, your, his, her, its, our, theirOur classroom is clean.
Quantifierssome, any, much, many, few, little, enoughWe have enough time.
Numbersone, two, first, secondTwo players scored.
Distributiveseach, every, either, neitherEvery child received a card.
Question determinerswhich, what, whoseWhich subject do you prefer?

4. Some and any

Use some most often in affirmative statements. Use any commonly in questions and negative statements.

  • There are some biscuits in the jar.
  • Are there any biscuits left?
  • There aren't any biscuits left.

However, some is also common in offers and requests when we expect or invite a positive response: Would you like some tea?

5. Much, many, few and little

Choose a determiner according to whether the noun is countable or uncountable.

WordUsed withExample
manyPlural countable nounsHow many pencils do you need?
muchUncountable nounsHow much sugar is left?
a few / fewPlural countable nounsI have a few questions.
a little / littleUncountable nounsAdd a little salt.

A few and a little mean some, enough to mention positively. Few and little stress that the amount is small or not enough.

  • We have a few minutes, so we can talk.
  • We have few minutes left, so we must hurry.
  • There is a little milk for tea.
  • There is little milk left, so we may need to buy more.

6. Common mistakes

IncorrectCorrectReason
She is an university student.She is a university student.“University” begins with a /y/ consonant sound.
I need an advice.I need some advice.“Advice” is normally uncountable.
Much students were absent.Many students were absent.“Students” is a plural countable noun.
The honesty is important.Honesty is important.No article is usually needed for this general idea.
These kind of books are useful.This kind of book is useful. / These kinds of books are useful.Make the determiner agree with the noun phrase.

7. Practice exercises

Try each question before opening its answer.

  1. Fill in the blank: I saw ___ elephant at the zoo.
    Show answer

    an — “Elephant” begins with a vowel sound.

  2. Fill in the blank: She is ___ honest person.
    Show answer

    an — The “h” in “honest” is silent.

  3. Choose the correct word: How (much / many) students are in your class?
    Show answer

    many — “Students” is a plural countable noun.

  4. Fill in the blank: Are there ___ questions?
    Show answer

    any — “Any” is commonly used in questions.

  5. Identify the determiner: Those flowers smell lovely.
    Show answer

    Those — It points to particular flowers.

  6. Correct the sentence: He gave me an useful tip.
    Show answer

    He gave me a useful tip — “Useful” begins with a /y/ sound.

  7. Choose the best option: There is (a few / a little) water in the bottle.
    Show answer

    a little — “Water” is uncountable.

  8. Choose the correct sentence: (The children need sleep / Children need sleep) when talking generally.
    Show answer

    Children need sleep. — No article is needed when referring to children generally.

8. Quick revision

  • A comes before a consonant sound; an comes before a vowel sound.
  • The points to a particular person or thing, or something understood as unique in context.
  • Determiners include demonstratives, possessives, quantifiers, numbers and question words used before nouns.
  • Use many/few with plural countable nouns and much/little with uncountable nouns.
  • Use no article in many general statements about plural nouns and uncountable nouns.
