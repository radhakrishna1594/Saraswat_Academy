Interrogative Sentences: Rules and Examples

An interrogative sentence asks a question. Learn how to form yes/no questions, use question words, arrange words correctly and avoid common errors in everyday English.

1. What is an interrogative sentence?

An interrogative sentence is a sentence used to ask a question. In writing, a direct question usually ends with a question mark (?).

  • Are you ready?
  • Where do you live?
  • Did she call you?
  • How wonderful was the performance? (A question asking about degree or manner.)

Questions may ask for a yes/no answer, specific information, confirmation or a choice.

2. Yes/no questions

These questions can usually be answered with yes or no. When a helping verb is present, it normally comes before the subject.

PatternExample
Be verb + subject ...?Are you tired?
Do/Does + subject + V1 ...?Does she like music?
Did + subject + V1 ...?Did they arrive early?
Have/Has + subject + V3 ...?Have you finished your work?
Modal + subject + V1 ...?Can he swim?
Will + subject + V1 ...?Will you join us?

Short answers repeat the helping verb: Yes, I am. / No, I'm not. / Yes, she does. / No, they didn't.

3. Questions with do, does and did

Use do with I, you, we and they in the simple present; use does with he, she and it. Use did for most simple past questions. The main verb remains in its base form (V1).

StatementQuestion
You play chess.Do you play chess?
Riya reads daily.Does Riya read daily?
He went home.Did he go home?

Do not use do/does/did with a main verb be when be itself forms the question: say Is she ready?, not Does she be ready?

4. Wh-questions

Wh-questions ask for specific information. Common question words include who, whom, whose, what, which, when, where, why and how.

Question wordAsks aboutExample
WhoA personWho is at the door?
WhatA thing or actionWhat are you reading?
WhereA placeWhere does he work?
WhenA timeWhen will the bus arrive?
WhyA reasonWhy are you laughing?
WhichA choiceWhich colour do you prefer?
WhoseOwnershipWhose bag is this?
HowManner, condition or degreeHow did you solve it?

A common pattern is question word + helping verb + subject + main verb: Where do they live? However, when the question word is the subject, do not add do/does/did: Who broke the glass?

5. Questions about choices

Choice questions offer two or more options. They often use or.

  • Would you like tea or coffee?
  • Are we meeting on Monday or Tuesday?
  • Which book do you want, this one or that one?

The answer normally selects one of the options rather than simply saying yes or no.

6. Question tags

A question tag is a short question added to a statement to ask for confirmation.

  • You are a student, aren't you?
  • She can drive, can't she?
  • They didn't call, did they?
  • He likes football, doesn't he?

As a general rule, a positive statement takes a negative tag, and a negative statement takes a positive tag. The tag usually repeats the sentence's helping verb.

7. Direct and indirect questions

A direct question asks something directly and usually uses question word order.

  • Direct: Where is the station?
  • Indirect: Could you tell me where the station is?

In an indirect question, the words after the opening phrase usually follow statement word order: Do you know what time it is? not Do you know what time is it?

Indirect questions are often more polite. When the whole sentence is a question, it ends with a question mark.

8. Common mistakes

IncorrectCorrectReason
Where you are going?Where are you going?Put the helping verb before the subject.
Does she likes tea?Does she like tea?Use V1 after does.
Did he went home?Did he go home?Use V1 after did.
What you want?What do you want?Most simple-present questions need do/does.
Do you are ready?Are you ready?Move the be verb before the subject.
Do you know where is he?Do you know where he is?Use statement word order in an indirect question.

10. Quick revision

  • Interrogative sentences ask questions and direct questions end with a question mark.
  • Move a helping verb before the subject for most yes/no questions.
  • Use do/does + V1 for simple present questions and did + V1 for simple past questions.
  • Use question words to ask for specific information.
  • When the question word is the subject, say Who called?, not Who did call? in a neutral ordinary question.
  • Indirect questions generally use statement word order after the opening phrase.
