Question Formation and WH-Questions

Questions help us ask for information, clarify ideas and keep conversations going. Learn the correct word order for yes/no questions and how to use who, what, where, when, why, which and how.

1. What Is an Interrogative Sentence?

An interrogative sentence asks a question and normally ends with a question mark. Questions may ask for a yes/no answer or for specific information.

  • Yes/no: Do you play cricket?
  • Information: Where do you play cricket?
  • Choice: Would you like tea or coffee?

The word order in a question is often different from the word order in a statement. Learning the pattern makes question formation easier.

2. Questions with “Be” Verbs

When the statement uses am, is, are, was or were, place the be verb before the subject to form a yes/no question.

StatementQuestion
She is tired.Is she tired?
They are at school.Are they at school?
He was late.Was he late?
You were busy.Were you busy?
I am correct.Am I correct?

Do not add do when the be verb itself can move before the subject. “Is she happy?” is correct; “Does she be happy?” is not.

3. Questions in the Simple Present

Use do with I, you, we and they, and use does with he, she and it. The main verb stays in its base form.

  • You like music. → Do you like music?
  • They live here. → Do they live here?
  • She speaks Hindi. → Does she speak Hindi?
  • Ravi plays chess. → Does Ravi play chess?

Notice that the -s ending disappears from the main verb when does is used: plays becomes does…play.

4. Questions in the Simple Past

Use did for most simple past questions. The main verb returns to its base form.

  • She visited Jaipur. → Did she visit Jaipur?
  • They watched the match. → Did they watch the match?
  • He bought a book. → Did he buy a book?

Avoid using the past form after did: “Did he went?” is incorrect; say “Did he go?” The verb be is different: “Was he there?” and “Were they ready?”

5. Questions with Modal Verbs and Other Auxiliaries

With modal verbs such as can, could, will, would, should, may and must, place the modal before the subject.

  • You can swim. → Can you swim?
  • She will join us. → Will she join us?
  • We should leave. → Should we leave?

With perfect and continuous tenses, move the first auxiliary before the subject.

  • They have finished. → Have they finished?
  • He is sleeping. → Is he sleeping?
  • She has been working. → Has she been working?

6. WH-Question Words

WH-questions ask for particular information. How is included in this group even though it does not begin with “wh”.

WordWhat it asks aboutExample
WhoA personWho is your teacher?
WhomA person as the object (formal)Whom did you invite?
WhoseOwnershipWhose bag is this?
WhatA thing, action or informationWhat do you need?
WhichA choice from a setWhich colour do you prefer?
WhereA placeWhere do you live?
WhenA timeWhen does the train leave?
WhyA reasonWhy are you laughing?
HowA way, condition or mannerHow did you solve it?

7. The Basic WH-Question Pattern

When the WH-word is not the subject, a common pattern is:

WH-word + auxiliary verb + subject + main verb + remaining words?

  • Where do you live?
  • Why did they leave early?
  • What is she reading?
  • When will we arrive?

Choose the auxiliary according to the tense and the verb used in the statement. Do not automatically use do if the sentence already has a suitable auxiliary.

8. When “Who” or “What” Is the Subject

When who or what asks about the subject doing the action, we usually do not add do, does or did.

  • Who called you? (Who performed the action?)
  • What happened yesterday?
  • Who lives next door?
  • What caused the problem?

Compare these questions:

  • Who broke the glass? — asks who performed the action.
  • Who did you call? — asks about the object; you performed the action.

This subject-versus-object distinction is an important key to forming accurate questions.

9. Questions with “How Many”, “How Much” and Other Phrases

WH-words can combine with other words to ask for more specific information.

  • How many books do you have? (Countable things)
  • How much water do we need? (Uncountable things or amounts)
  • How often do you exercise? (Frequency)
  • How long is the film? (Duration or length)
  • How far is the station? (Distance)
  • How old are you? (Age)
  • What time does the class begin? (Exact time)
  • How many times have you visited Delhi? (Number of occasions)

10. Indirect Questions: Keep the Word Order Correct

An indirect question is included inside a longer statement or question. After expressions such as Could you tell me…? and Do you know…?, use statement word order in the embedded question.

  • Direct: Where is the library? → Indirect: Could you tell me where the library is?
  • Direct: What time does the bus leave? → Indirect: Do you know what time the bus leaves?
  • Direct: Where did he go? → Indirect: I wonder where he went.

Do not keep the direct-question order inside the indirect question. “Could you tell me where is the library?” is not the standard form; use “Could you tell me where the library is?”

11. Negative Questions

Negative questions use a negative auxiliary such as don't, doesn't, didn't, isn't, aren't, can't or won't. They can express surprise, check an assumption or make a suggestion.

  • Don't you know the answer?
  • Isn't she coming today?
  • Didn't they receive the message?
  • Can't we try again?

The speaker's intention depends on context and tone. For a neutral information question, a positive form may sometimes sound more direct.

12. Common Errors in Question Formation

IncorrectCorrectReason
Does she likes tea?Does she like tea?Use the base verb after “does”.
Did he went home?Did he go home?Use the base verb after “did”.
Where you are going?Where are you going?Put the auxiliary before the subject.
Why she is upset?Why is she upset?Use question word order.
Who did call you?Who called you?“Who” is the subject in this question.
Can you to swim?Can you swim?Use the base verb after a modal.
Do you know where is he?Do you know where he is?Indirect questions use statement word order.

16. Quick Revision

  • With be verbs, auxiliaries and modals, place the verb before the subject in a yes/no question.
  • Use do/does in simple present questions and did in simple past questions when needed.
  • Use the base form of the main verb after do, does, did and modal verbs.
  • Use WH-words to ask about people, things, places, time, reasons, choices and manner.
  • If who or what is the subject, do not normally add do/does/did.
  • Indirect questions use statement word order after the question phrase.
  • End a direct question with a question mark.

When you are unsure, identify the tense, find the auxiliary verb and check whether the WH-word is the subject or the object. These steps will help you form questions accurately.
