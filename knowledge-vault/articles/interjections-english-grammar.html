Interjections in English Grammar: Meaning and Examples

Imagine opening a birthday gift and saying, “Wow! What a lovely surprise!” Or accidentally touching something hot and crying, “Ouch!” Words such as wow and ouch express a sudden feeling or reaction. They are called interjections.

1. What is an interjection?

An interjection is a word or short expression used to express a feeling, emotion, reaction or greeting. It often appears separately from the main sentence.

Hurray! We won the match.

Oh! I forgot my notebook.

Hello! How are you?

Ouch! That hurt.

Interjections are common in conversation, stories, dialogue and informal writing. In formal writing, use them only when they suit the tone.

2. Types of interjections

Feeling or purposeCommon interjectionsExample
Joy or celebrationHurray! Yay!Hurray! Our team has won.
SurpriseWow! Oh! What!Wow! That is an amazing painting.
PainOuch! Ow!Ouch! I bumped my knee.
Sorrow or sympathyAlas! Oh dear!Alas! We missed the last train.
DisgustYuck! Ugh!Yuck! This milk smells strange.
Calling attentionHey! Look!Hey! Wait for me.
GreetingHello! Hi!Hello! It is nice to meet you.
Hesitation or thinkingUm… Er…Um, let me think about that.

The same interjection can express different feelings depending on the situation and the speaker's voice.

3. Interjections and punctuation

An interjection can be followed by an exclamation mark, a comma or sometimes no separate punctuation, depending on how strong the feeling is and how it fits into the sentence.

PunctuationExampleEffect
Exclamation mark (!)Wow! That was fast.Strong or sudden feeling
Comma (,)Well, we can try again.A mild reaction or transition
Comma after greetingHello, Riya!Greeting within a sentence

Do not use exclamation marks after every sentence. Save them for excitement, strong emotion or a deliberate conversational effect.

4. Interjections in everyday conversation

Interjections can make dialogue sound natural, but the choice depends on the context.

  • “Oh, now I understand.” — realisation
  • “Oops! I sent the wrong file.” — a small mistake
  • “Bravo! You performed brilliantly.” — praise
  • “Phew! That was a close call.” — relief
  • “Shh! The baby is sleeping.” — asking for quiet

Some expressions, such as well and oh, can also have other grammatical roles. Their function depends on how they are used in the sentence.

5. Interjections and other parts of speech

An interjection often stands apart from the grammar of the sentence, while a conjunction connects ideas and an adverb gives more information about an action or description.

WordIts roleExample
Wow!Interjection: expresses surpriseWow! You finished early.
andConjunction: joins ideasI finished early and went home.
quicklyAdverb: describes an actionI finished the work quickly.

6. Common mistakes

  • Using the wrong tone: “Yay!” expresses happiness, while “Alas!” traditionally expresses sorrow or regret.
  • Too many exclamation marks: “Wow!!! Amazing!!!” can look exaggerated. One is usually enough.
  • Confusing a greeting and a sentence connector: “Hello!” greets someone; “and” connects words or clauses.
  • Ignoring context: “Oh” can express surprise, disappointment, understanding or many other reactions.

7. Practice questions

  1. Identify the interjection: “Hurray! We have finished the project.”
  2. Which interjection commonly expresses pain? (Ouch / Hurray)
  3. Complete the sentence with a greeting: “______! Nice to meet you.”
  4. Which word expresses disgust? (Yuck / Yay)
  5. Add suitable punctuation: “Wow that is beautiful”
  6. Choose an interjection expressing relief: (Phew / Alas)
  7. In “Oh, now I remember,” which word is the interjection?
  8. Does an interjection usually join two clauses in the way a conjunction does?
Show answers and explanations

  2. Ouch! — it is commonly used to express pain.
  3. Hello or Hi.
  4. Yuck! — it commonly expresses disgust.
  5. “Wow! That is beautiful.” A comma after “Wow” can also work if the sentence is read as one continuous expression.
  6. Phew! — it can express relief after difficulty or danger.
  7. Oh.
  8. No. Interjections express reactions; conjunctions such as and, but and because connect grammatical parts.
