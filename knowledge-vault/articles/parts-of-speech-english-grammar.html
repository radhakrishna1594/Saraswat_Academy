Parts of Speech in English Grammar

Every word in a sentence has a job. Some words name people or things, some show actions, and others describe or connect ideas. These jobs are called parts of speech.

Learn these word groups one at a time. The examples below use everyday situations, so you can see how grammar works in real sentences.

1. The eight parts of speech

Part of speechIts jobExamples
NounNames a person, place, animal, thing or ideaRiya, Jaipur, book, kindness
PronounUsed instead of a nounI, you, he, she, they
VerbShows an action or staterun, think, is, have
AdjectiveDescribes a noun or pronounbright, tall, blue
AdverbAdds information about a verb, adjective or another adverbslowly, very, often
PrepositionOften shows place, time or directionin, on, under, before
ConjunctionJoins words or ideasand, but, because, or
InterjectionExpresses a sudden feelingWow! Oh! Hurray!

A word's job depends on how it is used in the sentence. The same word can sometimes do different jobs.

2. Noun — a naming word

A noun names a person, place, animal, thing or idea.

Sentence: The teacher placed the book on the table.

Nouns: teacher, book, table.

More examples: doctor, Jaipur, rabbit, pencil, honesty.

3. Pronoun — a word used instead of a noun

A pronoun helps us avoid repeating the same noun.

Repeated: Aarav has a bicycle. Aarav rides Aarav's bicycle every day.

Clearer: Aarav has a bicycle. He rides it every day.

Common pronouns include I, me, we, you, he, she, it, they and them.

4. Verb — an action or state word

A verb tells us what someone or something does, or what state it is in.

SentenceVerb
The children laughed.laughed
My mother is a doctor.is
We have finished our work.have finished
The soup smells delicious.smells

5. Adjective — a describing word

An adjective gives information about a noun or pronoun. It can tell us what kind, which one or how many.

ExampleAdjectiveQuestion
a colourful birdcolourfulWhat kind?

6. Adverb — a word that adds information

An adverb can tell us how, when, where or how often something happens. It can also describe an adjective or another adverb.

SentenceAdverbQuestion
She spoke politely.politelyHow?
We leave tomorrow.tomorrowWhen?
Please wait outside.outsideWhere?
He often visits us.oftenHow often?
The test was very easy.veryHow easy?

Many adverbs end in -ly, but not all do. Often, very and well can also be adverbs.

7. Preposition — showing a relationship

A preposition often tells us where something is, when something happens or which direction something moves in.

PrepositionExampleRelationship
inThe keys are in the bag.Place
underThe cat is under the chair.Place
atClass starts at nine.Time
towardsThey walked towards the gate.Direction

8. Conjunction — joining words and ideas

A conjunction connects words, phrases or clauses.

ConjunctionExampleUse
andRiya and Kabir are friends.Adds information
butI was tired, but I finished my work.Contrast
orWould you like tea or milk?Choice
becauseWe stayed inside because it rained.Reason

9. Interjection — expressing a feeling

An interjection is a word or short expression that shows a sudden feeling or reaction.

InterjectionExampleFeeling
Wow!Wow! Your painting is beautiful.Surprise or admiration
Ouch!Ouch! That hurt.Pain
Hurray!Hurray! We won the match.Joy
Oh!Oh! I forgot my notebook.Realisation

10. Look at a complete sentence

Read: “Wow! The little boy quickly ran into the garden, but he forgot his ball.”

Words such as the and his are often classified as articles or determiners in modern grammar. The traditional eight-part list is a helpful school-level starting point.

11. A word can do different jobs

SentenceWordIts job
I drink water.waterNoun
Please water the plants.waterVerb
A fast train arrived.fastAdjective
The train moves fast.fastAdverb

That is why you should study words in complete sentences rather than memorising lists alone.

12. Practice questions

  1. Identify the nouns: “The puppy chased the ball.”
  2. Identify the pronoun: “They are waiting for the bus.”
  3. Find the verb: “My sister paints beautifully.”
  4. Find the adjective: “We sat under a shady tree.”
  5. Find the adverb: “The old man walked slowly.”
  6. Find the preposition: “Your shoes are beside the door.”
  7. Find the conjunction: “I wanted to play, but it rained.”
  8. Find the interjection: “Hurray! Our team has won.”
  9. Identify “brightly” and “lamp” in “The lamp shines brightly.”
  10. Find the verb: “Please close the window.”
Show answers
  1. puppy, ball
  2. They
  3. paints
  4. shady
  5. slowly
  6. beside
  7. but
  8. Hurray!
  9. brightly — adverb; lamp — noun
  10. close
