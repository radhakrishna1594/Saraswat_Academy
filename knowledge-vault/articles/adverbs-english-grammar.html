Adverbs in English Grammar: Types and Examples

Compare these sentences: “The tortoise walked” and “The tortoise walked slowly.” The second sentence tells us more about the action. The word slowly is an adverb. Adverbs can tell us how, when, where or how often something happens, and they can also change the meaning of adjectives and other adverbs.

1. What is an adverb?

An adverb is a word that often gives more information about a verb, an adjective, another adverb or sometimes a whole sentence.

SentenceWhat the adverb describes
She spoke softly.The verb spoke: how she spoke
The tea is very hot.The adjective hot: how hot
He ran quite quickly.The adverb quickly: to what degree
Fortunately, nobody was hurt.The whole statement

2. Adverbs of manner: How?

Adverbs of manner explain how an action is done. Many are formed by adding -ly to an adjective.

AdjectiveAdverbExample
carefulcarefullyPlease drive carefully.
quietquietlyThe baby slept quietly.
happyhappilyThey played happily.
goodwellShe sings well.
fastfastThe horse ran fast.

Spelling note: adjectives ending in consonant + y often change to -ily: easy → easily. Some adverbs do not end in -ly, such as fast, hard, late and well.

3. Adverbs of time: When?

Adverbs of time tell us when something happens or how long it lasts.

  • We will leave soon.
  • I met her yesterday.
  • He has already finished his work.
  • They waited all day.

Other examples include now, later, today, recently, still and finally.

4. Adverbs of place: Where?

Adverbs of place tell us where an action happens or in which direction someone or something moves.

AdverbExample
herePlease sit here.
thereKeep your bag there.
outsideThe children are playing outside.
nearbyWe found a shop nearby.
awayThe bird flew away.

5. Adverbs of frequency: How often?

These adverbs tell us how often something happens.

Frequency wordExample
alwaysShe always checks her answers.
usuallyWe usually eat at home.
oftenHe often visits his grandparents.
sometimesI sometimes walk to school.
rarelyThey rarely miss a class.
neverWater never burns in ordinary cooking conditions.

Adverbs of frequency usually come before the main verb (“I often read”) but after the verb be (“She is always polite”). With an auxiliary verb, they often come after the first auxiliary: “I have never seen it.”

6. Adverbs of degree: How much or to what extent?

Adverbs of degree tell us how strong, complete or limited something is.

  • The water is very cold.
  • I am almost ready.
  • The task was quite easy.
  • She was too tired to continue.
  • We have enough time.

Be careful with too and very. “Very hot” means the temperature is high. “Too hot” means it is hotter than is suitable for a purpose.

7. Interrogative adverbs

Interrogative adverbs help us ask questions about time, place, reason or manner.

WordQuestion
whenWhen will the train arrive?
whereWhere do you live?
whyWhy are you laughing?
howHow did you solve the problem?

8. Adjectives and adverbs: Do not mix them up

An adjective usually describes a noun or pronoun. An adverb often describes a verb, adjective or another adverb.

AdjectiveAdverbCorrect example
carefulcarefullyHe is a careful writer. He writes carefully.
goodwellThat is a good answer. You answered well.
hardhardIt is hard work. She works hard.
latelateThe late bus arrived late.

Common mistake: “She sings beautiful” should be “She sings beautifully.” But say “She has a beautiful voice,” because beautiful describes the noun voice.

9. Where should an adverb go?

Adverb position depends on the type of adverb and the meaning you want. These are common patterns:

  • After the verb or object: He completed the task quickly.
  • Before an adjective: The question is extremely difficult.
  • Before the main verb: They often visit us.
  • At the beginning for emphasis: Luckily, we caught the bus.

Do not place an adverb wherever it sounds convenient. Read the whole sentence to check that the meaning remains clear.

10. Practice questions

  1. Identify the adverb: “The baby slept peacefully.”
  2. What kind of adverb is yesterday in “We arrived yesterday”?
  3. Complete with an adverb of frequency: “I ______ brush my teeth twice a day.”
  4. Choose the correct word: “She answered (polite / politely).”
  5. Identify the adverb of degree: “The bag is very heavy.”
  6. Make an adverb from happy.
  7. Correct the sentence: “He drives careless.”
  8. Which interrogative adverb asks for a reason?
Show answers and explanations
  1. peacefully — it tells us how the baby slept.
  2. Adverb of time — it tells us when we arrived.
  3. Answers may vary: always, usually or often, depending on the intended meaning.
  4. politely — it describes how she answered.
  5. very — it tells us the degree of heaviness.
  6. happily.
  7. “He drives carelessly.”
  8. why.
