Present Perfect Tense: Rules and Examples

The present perfect tense connects a past action or experience with the present. Its basic structure is has/have + V3 (past participle). Learn when to use it and how to form statements, negatives and questions.

1. What is the present perfect tense?

We use the present perfect tense for past actions or experiences that are connected to now. The exact time is often not stated or is not the main point.

  • I have finished my homework. (It is finished now.)
  • She has visited Delhi twice. (Experience in her life so far.)
  • They have lived here for five years. (They still live here.)
  • He has lost his keys. (He does not have them now.)

Structure: Subject + has/have + V3 (past participle).

2. Affirmative sentences

SubjectHelping verbExample
I / You / We / TheyhaveWe have completed the work.
He / She / It / singular nounhasRiya has completed the work.

Use has with he, she, it and singular nouns. Use have with I, you, we and they. The main verb must be in its V3 form.

3. Negative sentences

Add not after has or have.

PositiveNegative
I have seen the film.I have not (haven't) seen the film.
He has arrived.He has not (hasn't) arrived.
They have eaten.They have not (haven't) eaten.

4. Questions

Move has or have before the subject to form a yes/no question.

StatementQuestion
You have finished.Have you finished?
She has arrived.Has she arrived?
They have seen it.Have they seen it?

Question-word examples:

  • Where have you put my notebook?
  • How many books has she read?
  • Why have they left early?

Short answers: Yes, I have. / No, I haven't. / Yes, he has. / No, he hasn't.

5. When do we use the present perfect?

Experiences up to now

Use it to talk about life experiences without specifying exactly when they happened.

  • I have travelled by train many times.
  • She has never ridden a horse.

Recent actions with a present result

  • Someone has broken the window. (The window is broken now.)
  • I have just cleaned my room.

Actions in an unfinished time period

  • We have had two tests this week. (The week is still in progress.)
  • He has called me three times today. (Today is not over.)

Situations that began in the past and continue now

  • I have known her for many years.
  • They have lived here since 2021.

6. Common time expressions

ExpressionExampleNote
alreadyI have already finished.Earlier than expected or by now.
justShe has just arrived.A very recent action.
yetHave you finished yet?Common in questions and negatives.
everHave you ever seen snow?At any time in your life so far.
neverHe has never flown in a plane.Not at any time up to now.
sinceWe have lived here since 2020.Starting point.
forWe have lived here for six years.Duration.
so far / recentlyI have read three chapters so far.Up to now / in the recent period.

Since gives the starting point (since Monday); for gives the length of time (for two days).

7. Present perfect or simple past?

Use the simple past when a completed past time is stated, such as yesterday, last year or in 2022. Use the present perfect when the time is unfinished, not specified, or the present result is important.

Present perfectSimple past
I have seen that film. (Experience; no time given.)I saw that film yesterday. (Specific past time.)
She has visited Jaipur three times. (Up to now.)She visited Jaipur in 2024. (Finished time.)
We have finished our work. (It is finished now.)We finished our work at six. (Past time stated.)

In standard English, do not usually combine the present perfect with a finished-time expression such as yesterday or last Monday.

8. Common mistakes

IncorrectCorrectReason
She have finished.She has finished.Use has with she.
I have went home.I have gone home.Use V3 after have.
He has ate lunch.He has eaten lunch.“Eaten” is V3 of eat.
Did you have seen it?Have you seen it?Do not add did to a present perfect question.
I have seen him yesterday.I saw him yesterday.Use simple past with a finished time.
She has lived here since five years.She has lived here for five years.Use for with a duration.

10. Quick revision

  • Structure: subject + has/have + V3.
  • Use has with he, she, it and singular nouns; use have with I, you, we and they.
  • Use the tense for experiences, recent results, unfinished time periods and situations continuing to now.
  • Use since for a starting point and for for a duration.
  • Use the simple past with finished past times such as yesterday and last year.
