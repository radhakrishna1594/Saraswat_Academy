Simple Future Tense: Rules and Examples

The simple future tense helps us talk about actions, decisions, promises and predictions about the future. Learn how to use will + the base form of a verb in statements, negatives and questions.

1. What is the simple future tense?

The simple future tense describes an action or situation that is expected, planned as a decision, promised or predicted to happen after the present time.

  • I will call you tomorrow.
  • Our team will win the match.
  • She will help her younger brother.
  • They will arrive in the evening.

Common time expressions include tomorrow, next week, soon, later, in a few days and in the future.

2. Affirmative sentences

Use will + V1 (the base form of the verb) with every subject. The verb does not change for he, she, it, I, we, you or they.

StructureExample
Subject + will + V1I will study tonight.
Subject + will + V1She will study tonight.
Subject + will + V1They will study tonight.

Contractions: I will = I'll; you will = you'll; he will = he'll; she will = she'll; we will = we'll; they will = they'll.

3. Negative sentences

To make a negative sentence, use will not + V1. The short form of will not is won't.

StructureExample
Subject + will not + V1I will not forget your advice.
Subject + won't + V1He won't attend the meeting.
Subject + won't + V1We won't be late.

Do not add do or does before will: say She will not come, not She doesn't will come.

4. Interrogative sentences

For a yes/no question, place will before the subject.

StructureExample
Will + subject + V1?Will you join us?
Will + subject + V1?Will Riya finish her work?
Will + subject + V1?Will they arrive on time?

Short answers: Yes, I will. / No, I won't.

Question words come before will: When will you return? Where will they stay? What will she cook?

5. Uses of the simple future tense

Predictions

  • I think it will rain tonight.
  • Technology will change many jobs.

Decisions made at the moment of speaking

  • The phone is ringing. I will answer it.
  • You look tired. I will carry your bag.

Promises and offers

  • I will always support you.
  • We will help you with the project.

Requests

  • Will you please open the window?
  • Will you help me with this question?

6. Will and shall

In modern English, will is commonly used with all subjects to talk about the future. Shall is still used in some questions with I and we when making an offer or suggestion.

SentencePurpose
Will you come tomorrow?Asking about a future action
Shall I open the door?Offering to do something
Shall we start the lesson?Making a suggestion

For most everyday future statements, using will is a clear and natural choice.

7. Simple future, going to and present continuous

English has more than one way to refer to the future. These forms often overlap, but they can express different ideas.

FormCommon useExample
Will + V1Instant decision, promise or predictionI will call you later.
Am/is/are going to + V1Prior plan or evidence-based predictionLook at those clouds. It is going to rain.
Present continuousArranged future eventWe are meeting the teacher tomorrow.

These are useful general patterns, not absolute rules. Choose the form that best expresses your intended meaning.

8. Common mistakes

IncorrectCorrectReason
She will goes home.She will go home.Use V1 after will.
I will to call you.I will call you.Do not use “to” after will.
He don't will come.He will not come.Use will not or won't.
Will she comes?Will she come?Use V1 after the subject in a will-question.
They will went there.They will go there.Use the base form, not V2.
Did you will help?Will you help?Do not use did with will.

9. Practice exercises

Try each question before opening its answer.

  1. Complete: I ___ (visit) my aunt tomorrow.
    Show answer

    I will visit my aunt tomorrow.

  2. Make negative: She will buy a new notebook.
    Show answer

    She will not (won't) buy a new notebook.

  3. Make a question: They will join us.
    Show answer

    Will they join us?

  4. Correct: He will studies tonight.
    Show answer

    He will study tonight.

  5. Complete: I think our school ___ (win) the competition.
    Show answer

    I think our school will win the competition.

  6. Choose: We (will / will to) meet you later.
    Show answer

    will — say “We will meet you later.”

  7. Complete the offer: ___ I carry your books?
    Show answer

    Shall I carry your books?

  8. Make a question with “when”: You will finish the work tomorrow.
    Show answer

    When will you finish the work?

  9. Correct: She doesn't will attend the class.
    Show answer

    She will not (won't) attend the class.

  10. Complete: Don't worry. I ___ (help) you.
    Show answer

    Don't worry. I will help you.

10. Quick revision

  • Affirmative: Subject + will + V1.
  • Negative: Subject + will not / won't + V1.
  • Question: Will + subject + V1?
  • Use will for predictions, instant decisions, promises, offers and requests.
  • Use the base form after will: will go, not will goes or will went.
  • Use shall I...? or shall we...? for offers and suggestions when appropriate.
