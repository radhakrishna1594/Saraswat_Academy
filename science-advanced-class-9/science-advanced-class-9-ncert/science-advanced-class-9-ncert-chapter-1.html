Chapter Overview

Measurement is the foundation of quantitative science. This chapter explains measurement as comparison with a standard unit, introduces CGS, FPS, MKS and SI systems, explains the need for common units, and develops unit conversion.

Key relation: Q = n × u, where Q is the physical quantity, n is its numerical value and u is its unit. If the unit changes, the numerical value changes so that the physical quantity remains unchanged.

Quick Check — Solutions

1. Name any two systems of units.

Answer: Two systems of units are the CGS system and the FPS system. Other systems discussed in the chapter are MKS and SI.

2. Why is SI system preferred over other systems?

Answer: The SI system is preferred because it is internationally accepted, easy to use and based on a decimal system. It provides a common language for measurement in science, trade and everyday life.

3. Convert 250 N into g cm/s2.

Solution: 1 N = 1 kg m/s2 = 1000 g × 100 cm/s2 = 105 g cm/s2.

Therefore, 250 N = 250 × 105 = 2.5 × 107 g cm/s2 (dyne).

4. Convert 1000 kg/L into kg/m3.

Solution: 1 L = 10−3 m3. Therefore,

1000 kg/L = 1000 kg ÷ 10−3 m3 = 106 kg/m3.

Check Your Understanding — Solutions

1. Which of the following is not an SI unit?

Answer: (d) foot. The foot belongs to the FPS system, whereas metre, kilogram and second are SI units.

2. The SI unit of mass is:

Answer: (b) Kilogram.

3. Name the system of units used internationally.

Answer: The International System of Units (SI) is the internationally accepted system of units.

4. Why is a common system of units necessary?

Answer: A common system avoids confusion and errors when measurements are communicated between people, countries and scientific communities. It also makes scientific data, trade and calculations easier to compare.

5. Why is measurement necessary in physics?

Answer: Physics deals with measurable physical quantities such as length, mass, time and temperature. Measurement gives these quantities numerical values and units, allowing observations, calculations and comparisons to be made accurately.

6. Why was there a need for a common system of units?

Answer: Different regions used different units, which caused confusion in communication, trade and scientific work. A common system was needed so that measurements could be understood and compared universally.

7. Explain the relation: Magnitude = Numerical value × Unit.

Answer: A measured quantity is expressed as a numerical value together with a unit. Thus, Q = n × u. For example, 5 m means the quantity is 5 times the standard unit metre.

8. Why does the same classroom floor give different numerical values when measured with sticks of different lengths?

Answer: The actual floor does not change, but the size of the chosen unit changes. A larger stick covers more length in one unit, so fewer units are required. A smaller stick requires more units. Hence numerical value is inversely proportional to unit size.

9. Why are numerical values different?

Answer: The sticks have different lengths: 1 unit, 2 units and 3 units. Therefore, the same wall is represented by different numbers of units. A larger unit gives a smaller numerical value.

10. Is the actual size of the classroom different? Why or why not?

Answer: No. The actual classroom remains the same. Only the unit used for measurement changes, so the numerical value changes while the physical quantity remains constant.

11. What conclusion can you draw about units and measurement from this activity?

Answer: The numerical value of a physical quantity depends on the unit chosen. If the unit is increased, the numerical value decreases in the same proportion, while the actual physical quantity remains unchanged.

12. Fill in the blanks.

(a) Measurement is the process of comparing an unknown quantity with a known quantity.

(b) The SI unit of mass is kilogram.

(c) In CGS system, the unit of length is centimetre.

(d) 1 km = 1000 m.

(e) The modern internationally accepted system of units is called SI.

13. Match the following.

Answer:

  • CGS → Centimetre-Gram-Second system
  • FPS → Pound (with foot and second as its fundamental units)
  • SI → International System of Units
  • MKS → Metre-Kilogram-Second system
  • Kelvin → SI unit of thermodynamic temperature

The table in the book is therefore matched by the corresponding system/unit: CGS → CGS system, FPS → Pound, SI → International system, MKS → Meter-Kilogram-Second, Kelvin → temperature unit.

14. What problems might occur if every country used its own system of units for measurement?

Answer: There could be confusion in international trade, errors in scientific calculations, difficulty in comparing measurements and problems in sharing scientific and technical information.

15. A scientist measures length in feet and another in meters. What difficulties may it lead to?

Answer: Their numerical measurements cannot be compared directly because the units are different. One measurement must first be converted into the other unit. Without conversion, calculations and communication may lead to errors.

16. If 1 meter was defined differently in different countries, what would happen to international trade?

Answer: International trade would become confusing and unreliable. The same stated length could represent different actual lengths in different countries, causing disputes, incorrect quantities and difficulty in comparing products and measurements.

17. A shopkeeper sells rice using kilograms. A foreign customer asks for rice in pounds.

(a) Why is unit conversion necessary here?

Unit conversion is necessary because the shopkeeper and customer are using different units for mass. Converting kilograms to pounds allows both to understand the same quantity.

(b) If 1 kg = 2.2 pounds, how many pounds are there in 5 kg?

5 kg = 5 × 2.2 = 11 pounds.

Important Formulae and Conversions

  • Q = n × u
  • n2 = n1(u1/u2)
  • 1 km = 1000 m
  • 1 m = 100 cm
  • 1 kg = 1000 g
  • 1 hour = 3600 s
  • 1 N = 105 dyne = 105 g cm/s2
